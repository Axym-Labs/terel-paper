\publicationabstract{%
Slow Feature Analysis learns representations that vary slowly without
collapsing or duplicating coordinates. Applying this objective to a deep
network usually introduces credit assignment across layers, observations, or
both. Temporal Regularized Learning (\TeReL{}) instead constructs a detached
regularized target at every layer and maps the target residual to the state of
each neuron. The feedforward gradient then factors into a presynaptic value
and this postsynaptic state. A single lateral matrix corrects the state once
and receives an anti-Hebbian state--state contribution. The resulting encoder
processes one observation at a time, sends no error signal across learned
layers or time, and stores three causal scalars per neuron together with one
matrix of auxiliary lateral parameters per layer. On a class-chunked MNIST stream,
batch-size-one training with stochastic gradient descent reaches
$\FinalTeReLAccuracy\pm\FinalTeReLSD$\% held-out linear-probe accuracy. Random
features reach $95.14\pm0.27$\%, and a layer-local supervised contrastive
network reaches $\FinalSupConAccuracy\pm\FinalSupConSD$\%. A spatially and
temporally relaxed reference, \TeReLOffline{}, reaches
$\FinalOfflineAccuracy\pm\FinalOfflineSD$\%. Shuffling the stream or removing
the temporal term reduces accuracy. Removing variance control produces a
nearly constant representation, whereas removing covariance control causes
numerical failure. Continued
unlabeled updates preserve held-out accuracy when their step size is selected
for a trained representation. Together, these results establish a simple deep
slow-feature rule for online and memory-constrained learning.}

\section{Introduction}

Temporal continuity supplies a learning signal before a task label is known.
When consecutive observations share a slowly changing cause, a representation
can suppress incidental variation by remaining close across time. Slow Feature
Analysis (SFA) formalizes this principle while preventing the constant solution
through variance and decorrelation constraints~\cite{wiskott2002}. Closely
related temporal-coherence objectives have long been used to learn visual
invariances~\cite{foldiak1991}, and modern self-supervised objectives likewise
combine agreement with variance or redundancy control~\cite{oord2018,zbontar2021,bardes2022}.

The optimization mechanism matters when observations arrive as a stream.
Backpropagation through a sequence retains an activation graph across time;
backpropagation through depth also transports a credit signal across learned
layers~\cite{rumelhart1986}. Predictive-coding and equilibrium formulations can
replace that graph with repeated neural dynamics, but then require iterative
settling~\cite{rao1999,scellier2017}. Both choices complicate continual
operation on hardware whose memory, communication, or update schedule is
constrained~\cite{indiveri2015,davies2018}. A learning rule local in time should
read only bounded detached state. A rule local in space should update a
synapse from quantities available at its two endpoints and within its layer.

Hebbian and anti-Hebbian networks give spatially local learning a useful
algebra: similarity-matching objectives yield Hebbian feedforward and
anti-Hebbian lateral rules for linear
representations~\cite{pehlevan2015,pehlevan2018}. A deep slow-feature rule
requires a richer
postsynaptic quantity: each neuron must combine temporal attraction, variance
preservation, and decorrelation before its synapses change. We construct this
quantity directly from a regularized target. For every observation and every
layer, the target residual is mapped through the local activation derivative
and corrected once by a same-layer matrix. The resulting signed value is the
neuron state used by both feedforward and lateral plasticity.

This construction yields \TeReL{}, a deep nonlinear encoder trained one
observation at a time. Adjacent learned layers are separated by a stop-gradient,
and all temporal references are detached. The feedforward update is an outer
product of presynaptic activity and the postsynaptic neuron state. One lateral
matrix serves both target decorrelation and state correction; its state--state
component is anti-Hebbian under the inhibitory sign convention. Running
moments, lateral parameters, and feedforward weights follow fixed-size leaky
recurrences. No optimizer moments are required by the reported online rule.

The empirical study separates the locality result from the performance made
available by relaxing it. \TeReL{} uses batch size one and two passes over the
training observations. \TeReLOffline{} retains a subsequence, evaluates the
soft-SFA loss only at the final layer, and backpropagates through both depth and
the subsequence. On MNIST, \TeReL{} improves over random features, a linear
input reference, batch SFA, and incremental SFA. It remains within 0.57
percentage points of layer-local supervised contrastive learning despite using
no labels in its objective. \TeReLOffline{} reaches the accuracy of a fully
offline supervised network. Matched interventions further show that the
temporal order and each regularizer affect the learned representation.

The paper makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a regularized activation target whose ReLU preactivation residual is a
  neuron-local postsynaptic state;
  \item a batch-size-one deep learning rule with Hebbian-factor feedforward
  gradients, an anti-Hebbian lateral contribution, one explicit same-layer
  correction, and one auxiliary matrix per layer; and
  \item a prespecified comparison with optimization-rich, supervised, random,
  and classical SFA references, plus matched mechanism tests.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Temporal Regularized Learning}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{One observation at one layer}

Consider observation $t$ at a layer with input $x_t\in\mathbb R^d$ and width
$D$. The layer computes
\begin{equation}
  a_t=W_tx_t+q_t,
  \qquad z_t=\phi(a_t),
  \label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
where $W_t\in\mathbb R^{D\times d}$, $q_t\in\mathbb R^D$, and $z_t$ is passed
to the next learned layer only after detachment. Before this observation, each
neuron stores a preceding activation $p_t$, a running mean $\mu_t$, and a
running variance $v_t$. The layer also stores a lateral matrix
$M_t\in\mathbb R^{D\times D}$. These quantities are fixed during the current
gradient calculation. Let $I_t\in\{0,1\}$ indicate whether $p_t$ belongs to
the same sequence, and write $\bar z_t=z_t-\mu_t$ and
$g_t=[\tau-v_t]_+$ for a variance target $\tau>0$.

The samplewise surrogate contains temporal, variance, and covariance terms:
\begin{equation}
\ell_T=\frac{I_t}{D}\lVert z_t-p_t\rVert_2^2
\quad
\ell_V=-\frac1D g_t^\top(\bar z_t\odot\bar z_t)
\quad
\ell_C=\frac1D\bar z_t^\top A_t\stopgrad(\bar z_t),
\qquad A_t=\offdiag(M_t).
\label{eq:local-losses}
\end{equation}
The layer minimizes
$\ell=\lambda_T\ell_T+\lambda_V\ell_V+\lambda_C\ell_C$ with
$\lambda_T>0$. The first term makes consecutive activations similar. The
second increases the coordinates whose stored variance lies below $\tau$.
The third applies a lagged same-layer decorrelation signal. Detachment makes
each term a function of the current activation and bounded stored quantities,
without a graph through earlier observations.

\subsection{The regularized target precedes the neuron state}

The three derivatives can be collected before a synapse is updated. Define
\begin{align}
r_t^{T}&=I_t(z_t-p_t),
&r_t^{V}&=-\frac{\lambda_V}{\lambda_T}g_t\odot\bar z_t,
&r_t^{C}&=\frac{\lambda_C}{2\lambda_T}A_t\bar z_t,
\label{eq:residual-components}\\
r_t&=r_t^{T}+r_t^{V}+r_t^{C},
&\widehat z_t&=\stopgrad(z_t-r_t).
\label{eq:regularized-target}
\end{align}
Thus $\widehat z_t$ is the activation preferred by the instantaneous balance
of slowness, variance, and decorrelation. It is formed within the layer; it is
not an external target.

\begin{proposition}[Activation-target equivalence]
\label{prop:target}
At the values used to construct \cref{eq:regularized-target},
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde\ell_z=\frac{\lambda_T}{D}
  \lVert z_t-\widehat z_t\rVert_2^2
  \label{eq:activation-target-loss}
\end{equation}
has the same derivative with respect to $z_t$ as $\ell$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The stored $p_t,\mu_t,v_t$, and $M_t$ are constants for differentiation.
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\nabla_{z_t}(\lambda_T\ell_T)&=\frac{2\lambda_T}{D}r_t^T,&
\nabla_{z_t}(\lambda_V\ell_V)&=\frac{2\lambda_T}{D}r_t^V,&
\nabla_{z_t}(\lambda_C\ell_C)&=\frac{2\lambda_T}{D}r_t^C.
\end{align*}
Their sum is $2\lambda_T r_t/D$. Since
$z_t-\widehat z_t=r_t$ at the construction point and the target is detached,
\cref{eq:activation-target-loss} has the same derivative.
\end{proof}

The activation residual must next be expressed at the input side of the
nonlinearity. Let
\begin{equation}
  b_t=J_{\phi,t}^{\top}r_t,
  \qquad
  J_{\phi,t}=\left.\frac{\partial\phi(a)}{\partial a}\right|_{a_t}.
  \label{eq:base-state}
\end{equation}
For the ReLU encoder used in the experiments, this is the explicit signed
quantity
\begin{equation}
  b_t=r_t\odot\mathbf 1[a_t>0].
  \label{eq:relu-state}
\end{equation}
No automatic-differentiation graph is needed to obtain it. A single
same-layer correction produces the postsynaptic neuron state
\begin{equation}
  s_t=b_t-\eta\kappa M_tb_t,
  \label{eq:state-correction}
\end{equation}
where $\eta>0$ is the correction step and $\kappa\geq0$ its strength. This is
one matrix--vector operation in the feedforward computation. It is not an
iterative settling procedure.

Define the detached preactivation target
$\widehat a_t=\stopgrad(a_t-s_t)$. Its local mean-squared error gives
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde\ell_a=\frac{\lambda_T}{D}
  \lVert a_t-\widehat a_t\rVert_2^2,
  \qquad
  \nabla_{W_t}\widetilde\ell_a=\frac{2\lambda_T}{D}s_tx_t^\top.
  \label{eq:feedforward-factor}
\end{equation}
Each feedforward synapse therefore receives a presynaptic scalar $x_{t,i}$
and a postsynaptic scalar $s_{t,j}$. This is the two-factor locality of a
Hebbian rule; the sign of $s_{t,j}$ determines potentiation or depression
under gradient descent. With learning rate $\alpha$ and coupled decay
$\lambda_W$, the weight recurrence is
\begin{equation}
  W_{t+1}=(1-\alpha\lambda_W)W_t
  -\alpha\frac{2\lambda_T}{D}s_tx_t^\top.
  \label{eq:weight-update}
\end{equation}
The corresponding bias update uses $s_t$ alone. The output of this layer is
detached before it becomes the input to the next learned layer, so no error
signal crosses the layer boundary.

\subsection{One lateral matrix and bounded causal state}

The same matrix supports the covariance signal in
\cref{eq:residual-components} and the state correction in
\cref{eq:state-correction}. After the synaptic update, form
\begin{equation}
  Q_t=(1-\omega)\offdiag(\bar z_t\bar z_t^\top)
      +\omega s_ts_t^\top,
  \qquad
  M_{t+1}=\rho M_t+(1-\rho)Q_t,
  \label{eq:lateral-update}
\end{equation}
with $\omega\in[0,1]$ and $\rho\in[0,1)$. The off-diagonal part of $M_t$ enters
the regularized target, while the full matrix enters the neuron-state
correction. If inhibitory lateral weights are written as $L_t=-M_t$, the
state component of \cref{eq:lateral-update} contributes
$-(1-\rho)\omega s_ts_t^\top$ to $L_t$: same-signed neuron states make their
mutual lateral connection more inhibitory, which is the anti-Hebbian sign.
The representation component preserves the decorrelation signal required by
the target.

The three causal scalars of a neuron follow
\begin{equation}
  p_{t+1}=z_t,
  \qquad
  \mu_{t+1}=\beta\mu_t+(1-\beta)z_t,
  \qquad
  v_{t+1}=\beta v_t+(1-\beta)(z_t-\mu_t)^2.
  \label{eq:causal-state}
\end{equation}
At a sequence boundary, only the validity of $p_t$ is cleared; the learned
moments and lateral parameters remain. A layer of width $D$ therefore stores
$3D$ causal scalars and one boundary-validity bit. It also carries $D^2$
auxiliary lateral parameters, separate from its feedforward weights. The
reported optimizer is plain stochastic gradient descent and adds no optimizer
state. For the $784$--$512$--$256$ MNIST encoder, these categories contain
$2{,}306$ causal scalars, $327{,}680$ auxiliary lateral parameters, and
$533{,}248$ feedforward parameters.

\subsection{The spatially and temporally relaxed reference}

\TeReLOffline{} measures what the same slow-feature principle can learn after
both locality constraints are relaxed within a subsequence. A complete
subsequence enters a ReLU multilayer perceptron; the soft-SFA objective is
evaluated at its final learned layer, and backpropagation differentiates that
objective through all preceding layers and observations in the subsequence.
No normalization layer is used. Momentum SGD optimizes this reference, so its
update is neither neuron-local nor free of optimizer state. It is consequently
a performance reference, not an alternative definition of \TeReL{}.

\section{Relation to previous work}

SFA obtains invariant features by minimizing temporal variation under
normalization and decorrelation constraints~\cite{wiskott2002}. Incremental
SFA adapts the classical linear construction to streams but retains a
whitening stage and linear feature extraction~\cite{kompella2012}. Other local
networks derive linear slow-feature or similarity-matching algorithms with
Hebbian feedforward and anti-Hebbian lateral plasticity
~\cite{lipshutz2020,pehlevan2015,pehlevan2018}. \TeReL{} retains this synaptic
factorization while making the postsynaptic factor the signed residual of a
nonlinear regularized target at each layer.

Modern self-supervised methods also prevent invariant representations from
collapsing. Contrastive predictive coding uses temporal context and negative
examples~\cite{oord2018}; Barlow Twins and VICReg instead regulate variance and
cross-coordinate dependence~\cite{zbontar2021,bardes2022}. \TeReL{} uses a
samplewise soft-SFA analogue of these three forces. Its focus is the execution
of the resulting update with bounded temporal state, not a new large-batch
self-supervised loss.

Layer-local representation learning blocks the backward path between network
modules~\cite{lowe2019,xiong2020,illing2021,siddiqui2024}. The Layer-local
Supervised Contrastive (Layer-local SupCon) comparator in our experiments
trains each layer with a supervised contrastive objective derived from
\citet{khosla2020}. It removes cross-layer credit assignment but still uses
labels, minibatches, adaptive optimizer state, and batch normalization.
\TeReL{} instead constructs its target from temporal and same-layer state and
processes one observation per update.

Predictive coding and equilibrium propagation replace ordinary
backpropagation with local dynamics around an energy or prediction error
~\cite{rao1999,scellier2017}. Their recurrent settling differs from the single
explicit correction in \cref{eq:state-correction}. The correction can be
evaluated directly in one feedforward schedule, after which both synaptic
updates and all state recurrences are complete.

\section{Experimental design}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation tests three hypotheses. First, batch-size-one \TeReL{} should
learn a useful deep representation despite its locality constraints. Second,
the temporal order and each component of the regularized target should affect
that representation. Third, continuing the same unsupervised updates along an
evaluation stream should preserve the representation when the continuation
step is chosen for an already trained encoder.

\subsection{Data, selection, and readout}

MNIST contains $60{,}000$ training images and $10{,}000$ held-out images
~\cite{lecun1998}. We reserve a stratified $10{,}000$-image subset of the
training data for model selection. The remaining images train the encoder and
a linear probe. The probe reads the concatenation of both learned layers and
is evaluated on the held-out images. All reported central values are means and
sample standard deviations over five independently initialized evaluations;
paired comparisons use the same initialization indices and 95\% Student-$t$
intervals.

The \TeReL{} stream consists of randomly permuted class-homogeneous chunks of
length 16. A sequence boundary separates consecutive chunks. Class labels
therefore determine the order, as they do in supervised contrastive sampling,
but do not enter the target or either synaptic update. The encoder has widths
512 and 256, uses ReLU, and receives two passes over the encoder-training
observations: $100{,}000$ batch-size-one updates in total. The selected
configuration, probe, split roles, and evaluation indices were fixed from
validation results before held-out evaluation. Exact hyperparameters and raw
values appear in \cref{app:protocol}.

\subsection{References and interventions}

The performance references are chosen to expose distinct relaxations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \TeReLOffline{} uses the same $512$--$256$ architecture and final-layer
  soft-SFA principle, with subsequences of 128 and end-to-end backpropagation;
  \item Layer-local SupCon uses labels and a supervised contrastive objective
  at each learned layer;
  \item supervised offline uses labels and backpropagation through the complete
  network, while the linear input reference applies supervised logistic
  readout directly to the pixels;
  \item a random $512$--$256$ ReLU encoder receives the same concatenated-layer
  readout; and
  \item batch SFA and incremental SFA provide classical linear slow-feature
  references.
\end{itemize}
The methods differ in their intrinsic data and optimization requirements; the
comparison therefore reports representation quality rather than equal compute.
All downstream evaluations use the same split roles and linear-probe procedure.

Four matched interventions examine \TeReL{} itself. One randomly shuffles the
stream. Two set the temporal or variance coefficient to zero. The fourth sets
the covariance coefficient to zero. Online continuation begins from the same
trained parameters and the same fitted probe as fixed-encoder evaluation. Each
representation is emitted before the current observation updates the encoder,
so an observation can affect only later representations. The continuation
update receives no label. A validation-only scale audit selects the largest
continuation step that preserves fixed-encoder accuracy and effective rank.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{A useful representation from samplewise local updates}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/performance-mechanisms.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Representation quality and matched temporal effects.}
  \textbf{a}, Held-out linear-probe accuracy. Points show means and horizontal
  intervals show sample standard deviations. \textbf{b}, Paired accuracy
  changes relative to fixed-encoder \TeReL{}; intervals are 95\% Student-$t$
  intervals. Online continuation uses the validation-selected step.}
  \label{fig:performance}
\end{figure}

\TeReL{} reaches $\FinalTeReLAccuracy\pm\FinalTeReLSD$\% held-out accuracy
(\cref{fig:performance}a). The random encoder reaches $95.14\pm0.27$\%, giving
a paired gain of 1.25 percentage points with interval $[0.88,1.61]$. The gain
over a linear probe on the input is 3.94 points $[3.65,4.23]$. Batch SFA and
incremental SFA reach $89.52\pm0.13$\% and $88.70\pm0.09$\%, respectively.
The nonlinear depth and regularized local target therefore contribute beyond
both the input and classical linear slow-feature references.

Layer-local SupCon reaches $\FinalSupConAccuracy\pm\FinalSupConSD$\%, 0.57
points above \TeReL{}. This comparator uses class labels, minibatches, batch
normalization, and AdamW at every layer. The small accuracy difference is
therefore obtained under a substantially less restrictive learning signal.
\TeReLOffline{} reaches $\FinalOfflineAccuracy\pm\FinalOfflineSD$\%, while the
fully supervised offline network reaches
$\FinalSupervisedAccuracy\pm\FinalSupervisedSD$\%. Their proximity shows that
the soft-SFA principle itself can support the supervised reference accuracy
when subsequence and cross-layer optimization are available. The remaining
gap to samplewise \TeReL{} is associated with the locality constraints rather
than a weak offline objective.

\subsection{Temporal order and anti-collapse terms}

The temporal interventions in \cref{fig:performance}b isolate the contribution
of adjacency. Randomly shuffling the stream lowers accuracy by 1.31 paired
points, with interval $[0.92,1.69]$. Removing the temporal term lowers it by
1.84 points $[1.65,2.02]$. These two changes preserve finite representations
but remove the advantage supplied by class-consistent adjacency.

The variance and covariance terms control more severe failure modes. Without
the variance term, accuracy falls to $15.10\pm0.18$\%, and the median feature
variance is approximately $1.4\times10^{-5}$. The effective-rank statistic is
not informative in this limit because many coordinates retain similarly tiny
variance. Without the covariance term, all five evaluations become non-finite
before a representation can be measured. The three target components thus
play distinct roles: temporal agreement improves the ordered representation,
variance prevents a near-constant solution, and covariance stabilizes the
multidimensional code.

\subsection{Continued online updates require a post-training step size}

The encoder and its causal state can continue updating along the evaluation
stream without changing the fitted probe. Reusing the initial training step,
however, is too aggressive after the representation has formed: a
validation-stream diagnostic shows progressively larger losses across stream
blocks and a sharp rank reduction. Scaling the continuation step by
$10^{-3}$ preserves validation accuracy ($96.17$\% versus $96.18$\%) and
effective rank ($90.41$ versus $90.01$) while retaining nonzero feedforward
parameter change. The same scale is fixed before the held-out continuation
comparison in \cref{fig:performance}b. Across the held-out evaluations,
continued updates reach $96.43\pm0.15$\%, compared with
$96.39\pm0.19$\% for the fixed encoder. The paired change is $+0.05$ points
with interval $[-0.03,0.12]$; effective rank changes by $+0.57$
$[-0.04,1.18]$. The mean feedforward parameter change is nonzero
($\lVert\Delta W\rVert_2=0.047$). These results separate the ability to execute
the rule online from the optimization schedule required after convergence.

baseline. The linear-probe result is therefore accompanied by local class
structure distributed through the learned feature space.

neuron-local formulation.

\section{Limitations and future work}

The present performance evidence uses a constructed MNIST order. The labels
define adjacency but never enter a \TeReL{} target or synaptic update. Natural
streams contain several time scales and may require a different balance among
temporal, variance, and covariance signals; establishing such a result on
large video or sensor streams remains the most important empirical extension.

Continued adaptation also introduces a schedule beyond initial representation
learning. A small fixed continuation step preserves the trained code in the
current setting, but it does not improve it. Local rules that regulate this
step from neuron state, parameter drift, or a stability criterion would make
training and long-term inference coincide more fully.

Finally, a layer of width $D$ uses $D^2$ auxiliary lateral parameters. This is
comparable to the feedforward parameters of an equal-width layer and distinct
from causal state, but it may still dominate narrow-input or very wide
architectures. Sparse, low-rank, or structured lateral recurrences are natural
directions for constrained physical implementations. The current experiments
also use multilayer perceptrons; convolutional and recurrent architectures
would test whether the same target-state construction scales while preserving
its locality boundary.

\section{Conclusion and outlook}

\TeReL{} begins with a soft slow-feature objective and ends with quantities
available at individual neurons and synapses. A detached regularized target
defines a signed postsynaptic state. Presynaptic activity and this state give
the feedforward gradient, while one same-layer matrix supplies an explicit
state correction and receives an anti-Hebbian state--state contribution. The
complete update processes one observation at a time with three causal scalars
per neuron, one auxiliary matrix per layer, and no error signal across learned
layers or time.

The experiments show that this locality is compatible with a useful deep
representation. Samplewise \TeReL{} improves on random, input-linear, and
classical SFA references; the relaxed \TeReLOffline{} construction reaches the
fully supervised offline result. The natural destination is continual
representation learning on devices where observations arrive sequentially and
global credit assignment is expensive. Extending the rule to natural temporal
structure and adaptive long-term updates would turn the present local
construction into a more general learning substrate for such systems.

\section{Locality and operation accounting}

For a layer of width $D$ with input width $d$, the feedforward multiplication
costs $Dd$ multiply--accumulates. Forming the target and causal statistics is
linear in $D$. The lateral target and state correction each use a
$D\times D$ matrix--vector multiplication, and updating the lateral moment
uses a rank-one outer product. The persistent quantities are summarized in
\cref{tab:resources}; temporary activations are not persistent causal state.
