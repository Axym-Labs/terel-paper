\publicationabstract{%
Slow features can reveal latent factors that persist while individual
observations change.  Minimizing temporal variation alone, however, also makes
a constant representation optimal.  Slow Feature Analysis (SFA) excludes this
solution by preserving variance and decorrelating feature coordinates.  We ask
whether the same principle can train a deep nonlinear encoder without
propagating errors between layers.  Temporal Regularized Learning (\TeReL)
realizes SFA through three soft signals at each neuron: temporal coherence,
gated variance expansion, and lateral decorrelation.  Canonical \TeReL{}
retains gradients between adjacent activations within a short chunk;
\TeReL{}-S detaches the preceding activation and therefore needs only fixed
temporal state.

\section{Introduction}

Observations often change more rapidly than the causes that generate them.
Object identity can persist across changes in viewpoint and illumination; an
activity can persist across noisy wearable-sensor measurements; and a
dynamical state can evolve more slowly than its observation noise.  Temporal
proximity can therefore identify factors that remain stable without requiring
two manually designed views of each observation.  Yet temporal coherence alone
cannot define a useful representation: the constant map is perfectly slow.

Slow Feature Analysis (SFA) removes this degeneracy by minimizing temporal
derivatives subject to zero-mean, unit-variance, and decorrelation
constraints~\cite{wiskott2002}.  Regularized self-supervised methods use a
closely related decomposition.  They couple an invariance term to variance and
covariance penalties that prevent collapse~\cite{bardes2022,zbontar2021}.
These principles are established.  The unresolved question studied here is
how to realize them in a deep nonlinear encoder whose parameters receive no
error from later layers.  A second question is what additional restriction
makes the resulting rule executable with bounded temporal history.

Temporal Regularized Learning (\TeReL) assigns each layer its own soft-SFA
objective.  A temporal term attracts the current activation to the preceding
activation.  A gated variance term expands a coordinate while its detached
running variance remains below a target.  A dense lateral operator tracks
off-diagonal second moments and supplies a lagged approximation to the
same-batch covariance gradient.  Stop-gradient boundaries between layers
confine credit assignment to the layer that produced each activation.

We call the rule neuron-local in space because the update of a feedforward
weight may use its presynaptic activity, quantities at its postsynaptic neuron,
and signals delivered through same-layer lateral connections, but no error from
a later layer.  Neuron-local does not mean neuron-independent: the dense
lateral operator couples all units within a layer and requires $O(D^2)$ state
at width $D$.

Two variants separate this spatial locality from locality across time.
Canonical \TeReL{} retains the preceding activation in a short within-chunk
graph, so its update is neuron-local but not temporally online.  \TeReL{}-S
detaches the preceding activation and retains only fixed-size state.  Either
rule may be evaluated on a minibatch; batching does not define the spatial
locality claim.

The source of temporal order also determines the scope of the evidence.  Our
main experiment arranges MNIST training examples into label-homogeneous chunks.
Labels do not appear in the \TeReL{} objective, but they do construct its
temporal supervision.  This experiment therefore tests the learning rule under
a controlled, task-aligned temporal relation; it is not unlabeled
self-supervision.  We also test chronological wearable-sensor streams while
withholding activity classes from the encoder loss.  That secondary experiment
is inconclusive, so we do not infer that arbitrary natural adjacency reveals
task structure.

The paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formulate \TeReL{} as a deep neuron-local soft-SFA rule and specify
  its relation to SFA, Incremental SFA, Bio-SFA, VICReg, and gradient-isolated
  predictive, contrastive, and blockwise learning.
  \item We derive the exact activation and parameter gradients with and without
  temporal detachment, including the Jacobian of the within-layer normalizer.
  We specify the detached state updates, decompose the error of the lagged
  lateral proxy, and measure its alignment with the same-batch direction.
  \item Under constructed MNIST temporal supervision, canonical \TeReL{}
  attains 97.30\% accuracy, compared with 98.34\% for backpropagation with the
  same number of data presentations and 95.35\% for a normalization-matched
  random encoder.  Local SupCon and direct-covariance controls delimit what can
  be attributed to the soft-SFA construction.
  \item We train \TeReL{}-S one sample at a time with fixed state and no
  retained temporal graph.  We report parameters, optimizer state, dynamic
  state, peak memory, operation proxies, and throughput as separate quantities.
  \item We separate validation-based selection from test evaluation and report
  matched architectures and readouts, five evaluation seeds, raw results,
  uncertainty intervals, and artifact-level provenance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Temporal Regularized Learning}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{From constrained SFA to a soft local objective}

Let $x_t$ denote a temporal stream.  On a local minibatch $\mathcal B$, layer
$\ell$ computes
\begin{equation}
  a_t^{\ell}=W^{\ell}z_t^{\ell-1}+c^{\ell},\qquad
  h_{\mathcal B}^{\ell}=N_{\eta^\ell,\mathcal B}(a_{\mathcal B}^{\ell}),\qquad
  z_t^{\ell}=\phi(h_t^{\ell}).
\end{equation}
Normalization is not part of the \TeReL{} objective.  The map $N$ may be the
identity or a within-layer normalizer with affine parameters $\eta^\ell$.
We use training-mode BatchNorm in the canonical MNIST configuration because it
was selected empirically as an optimization choice; \TeReL{}-S instead uses
the identity or a bounded streaming normalizer.  BatchNorm operates separately
at each neuron coordinate but couples the examples in $\mathcal B$ at that
coordinate.  It therefore changes the within-batch dependency, not the path of
credit across neurons or depth.

SFA seeks features that vary slowly without becoming constant or redundant.
A standard formulation minimizes $\mathbb{E}\|z_t-z_{t-1}\|_2^2$ subject to
zero mean, unit variance, and decorrelated outputs~\cite{wiskott2002}.  \TeReL{}
retains these three functions but replaces the hard population constraints by
soft terms computed from detached running state.  The SFA criterion is prior
work; the contribution is its realization as a deep neuron-local learning rule.

Consider $B$ activations $z_t\in\mathbb{R}^D$ from one layer.  A Boolean flag
$b_t$ marks the first sample of a subject, recording, or constructed chunk.
We define $I_t=1-b_t$ and also set $I_t=0$ when no stored predecessor is
available.  Let $N_I=\sum_t I_t$.  Before minibatch $k$, the layer holds the
detached state $(m_{k-1},v_{k-1},A_{k-1})$.  Suppressing the minibatch index,
the local objective is
\begin{align}
L_S &= \frac{1}{N_ID}\sum_{t:I_t=1}\|z_t-p_t\|_2^2,\label{eq:similarity}\\
L_V &= -\frac{1}{BD}\sum_{t=1}^{B}\sum_{j=1}^{D}
  [\gamma-v_j]_+(z_{tj}-m_j)^2,\label{eq:variance}\\
L_C &= \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{t=1}^{B}(z_t-m)^\top A q_t,\label{eq:covariance}\\
L &= \lambda_S L_S+\lambda_VL_V+\lambda_CL_C.\label{eq:terel}
\end{align}
When $N_I=0$, both $L_S$ and its gradient are zero.  Canonical \TeReL{} sets
$p_t=z_{t-1}$ within a short chunk and $q_t=\stopgrad(z_t-m)$.  The input to
the next layer is detached, so the temporal graph cannot transmit an error
across depth.  \TeReL{}-S instead sets
$p_t=\stopgrad(z_{t-1})$, or uses the detached final activation from the
preceding call.  In both variants, a boundary flag removes the corresponding
temporal pair.

The negative sign in \cref{eq:variance} determines the action of the variance
term.  Treating $g_j=[\gamma-v_j]_+$ as fixed state, minimizing $L_V$ moves an
active coordinate away from its running mean until its variance reaches
$\gamma$.  Thus $L_V$ is a local gradient surrogate for the SFA variance
constraint, not the usual nonnegative scalar variance penalty of VICReg.  Its
behavior depends on the timescale of the running statistics, the normalizer,
and the activation.  We select these algorithmic hyperparameters on validation
data.

\subsection{Lateral decorrelation as a gradient proxy}

For minibatch $k$, the detached off-diagonal second-moment operator is
\begin{equation}
C_k=\offdiag\left(\frac{1}{B}\sum_t(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})
(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})^\top\right).
\end{equation}
Our direct-covariance control minimizes
$L_{\mathrm{direct}}=\|C_k\|_F^2/D$.  Because $m_{k-1}$ is detached, the
gradient with respect to $z_{k,t}$ is
$4C_k(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})/(BD)$.  \TeReL{} cannot use $C_k$ before the current
activations have been observed.  It therefore evaluates \cref{eq:covariance}
with the stored operator $A_{k-1}$.  Detaching $A_{k-1}$ and $q_{k,t}$ gives
the activation direction $A_{k-1}q_{k,t}/(BD)$.  When
$A_{k-1}\approx C_k$ and $q_{k,t}=z_{k,t}-m_{k-1}$, this direction agrees
with the direct gradient up to the positive factor four.  The relation is a
lagged gradient approximation, not an equality of the two scalar objectives.

A shifted variant sets $q_t$ to the preceding centered activation.  Writing
$u_t=z_t-m$, its deviation from the direct direction satisfies
\begin{equation}
\|Aq_t-Cu_t\|_2
\leq \|A-C\|_2\|u_t\|_2+\|A\|_2\|q_t-u_t\|_2.
\label{eq:shift-bound}
\end{equation}
The first term measures error in the lateral operator.  The second measures
error from substituting the preceding activation for the current one.  The
bound gives no guarantee that either error is small, so the shifted rule is
used only as an ablation.

\subsection{Neuron-local gradient dependencies}\label{sec:gradients}

Let $g=[\gamma-v]_+$.  For \TeReL{}-S, differentiating the three local terms
with respect to the post-activation gives
\begin{equation}
e_t=\frac{2\lambda_S I_t}{N_ID}(z_t-p_t)
-\frac{2\lambda_V}{BD}g\odot(z_t-m)
+\frac{\lambda_C}{BD}Aq_t.
\label{eq:local-gradient}
\end{equation}
Set $r_s=e_s\odot\phi'(h_s^\ell)$, and denote the $(s,t)$ block of the
normalizer's minibatch Jacobian by
$J_{st}^{N}=\partial h_s^\ell/\partial a_t^\ell$.  Because the input
$z_t^{\ell-1}$ to the learned layer is detached, the exact same-layer chain
rule is
\begin{equation}
u_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal B}(J_{st}^{N})^\top r_s,\qquad
\frac{\partial L^{\ell}}{\partial W^{\ell}}
=\sum_t u_t(z_t^{\ell-1})^\top,
\qquad
\frac{\partial L^{\ell}}{\partial c^{\ell}}
=\sum_t u_t,
\qquad
\frac{\partial L^{\ell}}{\partial\eta^\ell}
=\sum_s\left(\frac{\partial h_s^\ell}{\partial\eta^\ell}\right)^\top r_s.
\label{eq:weight-gradient}
\end{equation}
For $N=\mathrm{Id}$, $J_{st}^{N}=\delta_{st}I$, and the weight gradient reduces
to the presynaptic outer product
$\sum_t(e_t\odot\phi'(a_t^\ell))(z_t^{\ell-1})^\top$.  BatchNorm in training
mode instead mixes the output-side factors across examples in $\mathcal B$ and
also updates $\eta^\ell$.  For BatchNorm, this mixing remains within each
postsynaptic coordinate; the lateral term supplies the only cross-neuron
signal.  Autograd computes the same-layer Jacobian exactly.  Neither normalizer
admits an error from a later layer, but only the identity normalizer admits the
displayed per-example factorization.

Canonical \TeReL{} keeps $p_t=z_{t-1}$ in the graph within a short chunk.  The
temporal term then contributes
\begin{equation}
\frac{2\lambda_S}{N_ID}
\left[I_t(z_t-z_{t-1})-I_{t+1}(z_{t+1}-z_t)\right],
\label{eq:batch-gradient}
\end{equation}
where missing and boundary-crossing terms are omitted.  The second term depends
on $z_{t+1}$ and is therefore not temporally online.  Canonical \TeReL{} is
neuron-local in space; \TeReL{}-S is neuron-local in space and local in time.
Across minibatch calls, canonical \TeReL{} also reads its stored predecessor as detached state.
In the fixed MNIST construction, an average of 151.2 among 46,866 valid edges
per data presentation cross a minibatch boundary.  These edges constitute
0.32\% of the total, or 9,073 edges over the 60-pass encoder schedule.  Every
other valid edge uses the live within-call gradient in
\cref{eq:batch-gradient}.

\subsection{State update and execution order}

The loss for minibatch $k$ is evaluated with the state available at the start
of the step.  After the optimizer update, the resulting activations update the
state under \texttt{no\_grad}:
\begin{align}
m_k &= \rho_m m_{k-1}+(1-\rho_m)\bar z_k,\label{eq:mean-update}\\
v_k &= \rho_m v_{k-1}+(1-\rho_m)\frac1B\sum_t
(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})^2,\label{eq:var-update}\\
A_k &= \rho_A A_{k-1}+(1-\rho_A)\offdiag\left(
\frac1B\sum_t(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})^\top\right).
\label{eq:lat-update}
\end{align}
The variance and lateral updates both use the pre-step mean $m_{k-1}$.  When
$\rho_A=0$, \cref{eq:lat-update} writes $A_k=C_k$ after the loss for minibatch
$k$ has already used $A_{k-1}$.  The canonical lateral signal is therefore
lagged by one minibatch.  \TeReL{}-S also stores the final activation and its
centered value as detached temporal state.  No activation graph survives the
call.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{One optimization step of \TeReL{}-S for a deep encoder.}
\label{alg:terel}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require input batch/stream segment $X$, boundary flags $b$, layer parameters
  $\{W^\ell,c^\ell,\eta^\ell\}$, and states
  $\{m^{\ell},v^{\ell},A^{\ell},p^{\ell}\}$
\State $h\gets X$
\For{layer $\ell=1,\ldots,L$}
  \State $z^{\ell}\gets\phi(N_{\eta^\ell,\mathcal B}(W^{\ell}\stopgrad(h)+c^{\ell}))$
  \State construct detached predecessors $p_t^{\ell}$; remove pairs marked by $b$
  \State evaluate the local objective $L^{\ell}$ in \cref{eq:terel}
  \State $h\gets\stopgrad(z^{\ell})$
\EndFor
\State differentiate $\sum_{\ell}L^{\ell}$ within each layer, including
  $N_{\eta^\ell,\mathcal B}$, and update the parameters
\For{layer $\ell=1,\ldots,L$}
  \State update detached state by \cref{eq:mean-update,eq:var-update,eq:lat-update}
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

For the identity normalizer, the update of $W^\ell_{ji}$ factors into the
presynaptic activity $z^{\ell-1}_i$ and an output-side factor for neuron $j$.
That factor uses neuron $j$'s temporal and variance state together with the
signal delivered by row $j$ of the lateral operator.  The state updates are
local to the same graph: $m_j$ and $v_j$ are coordinatewise, while each
$A_{jk}$ uses the centered activities at the endpoints of that lateral
connection.  The BatchNorm variant
adds cross-example dependence at neuron $j$ but no cross-depth error.  We
therefore describe the spatial rule as neuron-local while allowing dense
same-layer communication.  Canonical \TeReL{} also propagates temporal
credit within a short chunk; \TeReL{}-S cuts this path and is online with
bounded history at batch size one.  The dependency graph alone implies neither
biological plausibility nor hardware efficiency.

\subsection{State and memory cost}\label{sec:cost}

At width $D$, the dynamic state of one \TeReL{} layer contains a mean vector,
a variance vector, a $D\times D$ lateral operator, the preceding activation,
the preceding centered activation, and one validity flag.  The total is
$D^2+4D+1$ elements.  A bounded streaming normalizer adds location and scale
vectors containing $2D$ elements.  This state is independent of stream length
but quadratic in layer width.

Encoder parameters, optimizer moments, batch activations, and temporary matrix
products are separate costs.  After its first update, AdamW contributes about
two parameter-sized moment buffers.  The lateral state is not needed for
inference, but it remains a training cost.  Batch-size-one execution removes
batch-dependent storage and temporal graphs; it does not remove the dense
lateral matrix.

\section{Relation to prior work}\label{sec:related}

Two lines of work meet in \TeReL{}.  SFA supplies the structure of the local
objective.  Deep local representation learning supplies the credit-assignment
setting.  \TeReL{} combines them through a particular soft objective and a
specified execution boundary; it does not replace either foundation.

\paragraph{SFA and incremental SFA.}
Temporal slowness, variance preservation, and decorrelation define the SFA
objective~\cite{wiskott2002}.  Linear batch SFA whitens the observations and
extracts minor components of their temporal differences.  IncSFA replaces
these batch operations with incremental PCA and minor-component updates that
never form a covariance matrix.  IncSFA also handles episodic boundaries and
has Hebbian and anti-Hebbian update forms~\cite{kompella2012}.  It is therefore
the relevant online precedent rather than an alternative implementation of
batch SFA.  Our
empirical baseline is a Python 3 port of the authors' published linear
algorithm.  We document its provenance and validate it against batch SFA on a
synthetic stream.  \TeReL{} instead applies a soft objective directly to each
learned nonlinear layer and pays for a dense tracked lateral operator.

PowerSFA trains deep SFA models end to end by differentiating through an
approximate whitening operation~\cite{schuler2018}.  Deep gradient-based SFA
therefore predates \TeReL{}.  The distinction is that \TeReL{} blocks gradients
between learned layers and, in \TeReL{}-S, across temporal history.  Temporal
coherence has also been used directly in deep encoders.  Slow and Steady
Feature Analysis, for example, regularizes first- and higher-order changes
across video tuples~\cite{jayaraman2016}.  We restrict attention to first-order
adjacency to study a neuron-local realization of soft constraints and bounded temporal
state.

\paragraph{Bio-SFA and trace learning.}
Bio-SFA derives an online neural network for SFA with local synaptic
updates~\cite{lipshutz2020}.  Thus neither online SFA nor local SFA is new here.
\TeReL{} differs in its deep soft-penalty parameterization and its explicit
stop-gradient implementation for standard nonlinear layers.  F\"oldi\'ak-style
trace learning also exploits temporal persistence~\cite{foldiak1991}.  Its
positive activation trace is algebraically distinct from the signed temporal
difference in \cref{eq:local-gradient}.

\paragraph{Gradient-isolated predictive and contrastive learning.}
Greedy InfoMax trains a stack of gradient-isolated modules with local InfoNCE
objectives that predict along the natural order of images, audio, and
video~\cite{lowe2019}.  It is the closest precedent for combining temporal
structure with greedy depth-local representation learning.  CLAPP makes
contrastive prediction causal and local across both depth and time.  Its update
uses pre- and postsynaptic activity, predictive dendritic input, and broadcast
modulation, but still requires positive and negative examples~\cite{illing2021}.
LoCo trains overlapping blocks with paired-view contrastive objectives.  The
loss of the next block can update parameters in the overlap, so LoCo relaxes
strict gradient isolation~\cite{xiong2020}.

Local temporal prediction and gradient-isolated deep learning are therefore
prior art.  \TeReL{} studies a different objective: a
noncontrastive soft-SFA loss with explicit variance and redundancy control.
\TeReL{}-S uses neither a negative set nor a predictive decoder, but it requires
a dense, lagged, same-layer operator.  Its main MNIST experiment also uses
labels to construct temporal order, unlike the principal unlabeled settings of
these predictive methods.

\paragraph{Regularized and blockwise self-supervision.}
VICReg and Barlow Twins prevent collapse without negative examples by
penalizing variance and covariance, or cross-correlation,
respectively~\cite{bardes2022,zbontar2021}.  Blockwise self-supervised learning
has shown that VICReg, Barlow Twins, and contrastive losses can train network
blocks without end-to-end backpropagation~\cite{siddiqui2024}.  \TeReL{} differs
in its temporal positive relation, detached running moments, and same-layer
covariance-gradient proxy.  These choices also permit one-observation-at-a-time
execution.

The execution details further separate the methods.  Siddiqui et al. find that
simultaneous block training is materially stronger than sequential freezing,
showing that forward co-adaptation remains useful after gradients
are stopped at block boundaries~\cite{siddiqui2024}.  Canonical \TeReL{} instead
uses greedy layerwise training.  Datta et al. apply VICReg losses to individual
layers using original--augmented image pairs and current-batch variance and
covariance terms~\cite{datta2025}.  \TeReL{} uses a temporal relation, a gated
detached variance state, and a lagged lateral gradient proxy.

The positive relation itself matters.  Spectral analyses show that it can
determine downstream geometry when it aligns with task labels
~\cite{balestriero2022}.  Our class-chunk MNIST experiment must therefore be
interpreted as constructed pair supervision and compared with a label-aware
local baseline.  It does not show that arbitrary natural adjacency reveals
class structure.

\section{Experimental protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

The experiments examine four properties of the learning rule.  The primary
comparison measures canonical \TeReL{} against backpropagation, two random
encoders, and a label-aware local learner under the same constructed temporal
supervision.  A readout comparison tests whether the final layer retains the
learned signal.  A samplewise experiment tests whether \TeReL{}-S can learn a
non-collapsed representation with bounded state.  Finally, a secondary
wearable-sensor experiment asks whether the temporal mechanism transfers to a
naturally ordered stream.

\subsection{Data splits and roles}

\paragraph{MNIST controlled temporal supervision.}
Using seed 1701, we stratify the official 60,000 MNIST training examples into
50,000 training and 10,000 validation examples.  The official 10,000-example
test split is reserved for final evaluation.  At each encoder epoch, the
training examples are randomly arranged into same-class chunks of length 16.
Every chunk start defines a temporal boundary.  Labels determine this order but
never enter the \TeReL{} objective.  The protocol isolates the effect of a
task-aligned temporal teaching signal and is not unlabeled self-supervision.

\paragraph{PAMAP2 secondary natural-order stress test.}
PAMAP2 provides 100-Hz wearable-sensor measurements and activity annotations
for nine subjects~\cite{reiss2012pamap2}.  We retain its 12 protocol
activities, remove transition label 0 as directed by the dataset documentation,
and downsample with stride 10.  Missing sensor values are imputed with means
from the training subjects, and all features are standardized with statistics
from those subjects.

Subjects 1--6 train the encoder and probe, subject 7 provides validation data,
and subject 8 is held out for testing.  Subject 9 is excluded before model
selection because its protocol recording contains only rope jumping and cannot
support a 12-class test.  Temporal boundaries occur at subject starts, gaps
created by the removed transition rows, and timestamp discontinuities.  The
encoder iterator returns only features and boundary flags.  Activity classes
do not enter its loss, although transition annotation 0 affects preprocessing
and boundary placement.  This experiment tests the transfer of the temporal
intuition; it does not replace the controlled MNIST comparison.

\subsection{Models, baselines, and readouts}

All primary encoders have dimensions
$784\rightarrow512\rightarrow256$ and use LeakyReLU with negative slope 0.01.
BatchNorm is a configuration choice rather than a component of the three-term
objective.  Canonical \TeReL{} uses affine BatchNorm with update momentum 0.1, live
temporal pairs within each chunk, and greedy training for 30 epochs per layer.
The encoder consequently receives 60 complete presentations of the training
set.  AdamW uses learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and
batch size 256.  The loss coefficients are
$(\lambda_S,\lambda_V,\lambda_C)=(1,2.5,1)$.  The population momentum is 0.9.
Setting $\rho_A=0$ writes the lateral operator after each optimizer step, so
the next minibatch uses the operator computed from the preceding minibatch.

The backpropagation baseline has the same hidden dimensions, activation,
normalization, optimizer, batch size, and 60 data presentations.  It is trained
end to end with cross entropy.  \TeReL{}-S detaches the temporal references,
trains both layers simultaneously with local objectives, omits BatchNorm, and
uses coefficients $(1,5,1)$.  Its separately validated schedule contains ten
rather than 60 data presentations.  We therefore report its resource use but
do not describe it as data-presentation-matched to backpropagation.

Two random encoders distinguish learning from normalization.  The first keeps
its seed-specific hidden weights at initialization and omits normalization,
matching the \TeReL{}-S architecture.  The second adds the affine BatchNorm
modules used by canonical \TeReL{}, but leaves its hidden weights, biases,
BatchNorm scales, and BatchNorm offsets at initialization.  A
single \texttt{no\_grad} pass over the 50,000 stored training examples, with
batch size 256, updates only the running means and variances.  The BatchNorm
update momentum is 0.1, equivalent to retention 0.9.  The encoder then enters
evaluation mode, and the all-layer probe extracts representations using these
fixed training-split statistics.  The calibration is data-dependent
normalization, not a parameter update.

Canonical minibatches concatenate randomly permuted, class-homogeneous chunks.
A minibatch can therefore contain several classes, and BatchNorm communicates
across all 256 examples in that minibatch.  During greedy training, only the
active layer's BatchNorm remains in training mode.  The first layer's running
statistics are fixed before training the second layer.  Representation
extraction and probing use the final running statistics and learned affine
parameters in evaluation mode.  BatchNorm changes the within-layer information
path, but it never transmits an error across depth.

Local SupCon provides a label-aware local comparison for the constructed MNIST
protocol.  Each layer minimizes a supervised contrastive loss over all
same-label positives in its minibatch.  Activations are detached between
layers, and both layers update simultaneously.  Local SupCon matches the
primary architecture, BatchNorm, batch size, 60 data presentations, all-layer
probe, and evaluation seeds.  Validation searches a $3\times2$ grid with
learning rates $\{10^{-4},3\times10^{-4},10^{-3}\}$ and temperatures
$\{0.1,0.2\}$.  The resulting six candidates equal the method-selection budget
used for \TeReL{}.

Every primary method is evaluated by the same linear classifier on the
concatenation of both hidden layers.  A second probe reads only the final layer
of the identical canonical \TeReL{} encoder and tests depth specialization.
All probes use AdamW for 60 epochs with learning rate 0.003, weight decay
$10^{-4}$, and batch size 2,048.  Matching readout access prevents the breadth
of the representation from being confounded with the training rule.

\subsection{Model selection and evaluation separation}

Model selection uses only the 10,000 validation examples and seeds 101, 202,
and 303.  After comparing candidate configurations on these seeds, we fix one
configuration for each method before the five-seed evaluation.  The Local
SupCon learning-rate and temperature grid, the \TeReL{} component interventions,
and the lateral-signal controls remain validation-only.  No reported test
result selects among alternative configurations.

Batch-size-one execution changes both normalization and the number of optimizer
updates, so the streaming configuration is selected separately.  It uses
\TeReL{}-S, bounded running normalization, two data presentations, learning
rate $6\times10^{-6}$, statistics and lateral momenta 0.99, and one forward and
backward step per example.  It accesses no future example and retains neither
a temporal graph nor a buffer that grows with stream length.

\subsection{Evaluation and uncertainty}

The main evaluation uses seeds 1101, 1202, 1303, 1404, and 1505.  For every
method, we report the raw seed values, mean, sample standard deviation, a
small-sample Student-$t$ interval, and a percentile 95\% bootstrap interval
from 10,000 resamples with analysis seed 260803.  Primary contrasts pair
methods by seed.  Student-$t$ intervals are the principal intervals; bootstrap
intervals provide a sensitivity analysis.  Evidence for a learned
representation is measured against both the normalization-matched and the
no-normalization random encoders.  A canonical seed is classified as
non-collapsed only if its effective rank is at least 20, its median variance is
at least 0.05, and its active fraction is at least 0.25.

The configurations, seeds, normalization calibration, and analysis rules are
fixed before evaluation.  Each selected method is evaluated once on the five
test seeds.  The BatchNorm-calibrated random control uses the same seeds without
a validation sweep.  Mechanism and direct-covariance analyses remain on the
validation split and cannot select replacement models.  The supplement
contains the full selection ledger and all raw outputs.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Competitive performance}

Canonical \TeReL{} attains $97.30\pm0.07$\% test accuracy.  Matched
backpropagation attains $98.34\pm0.08$\%, giving a paired gap of 1.04 percentage
points with 95\% Student-$t$ interval $[0.99,1.08]$.  Although the absolute
accuracy gap is small, \TeReL{} has 2.70\% error compared with 1.66\% for
backpropagation.  Its error rate is therefore 63\% higher.

The BatchNorm-calibrated random encoder attains $95.35\pm0.19$\%.  \TeReL{}
improves on this control by 1.95 points, with paired interval $[1.64,2.25]$.
The no-normalization random encoder attains $95.13\pm0.27$\%; calibration
changes the random-encoder mean by 0.22 points, with interval
$[-0.08,0.52]$.  Every \TeReL{} seed satisfies the non-collapse criteria, and
every intended layer and normalization parameter group receives a nonzero
update.  Thus a two-hidden-layer encoder trained by neuron-local soft-SFA reaches
97.30\% in the constructed MNIST regime.

\subsection{Mechanism isolation}

The validation-only mechanism study changes one factor at a time under the
canonical protocol.  Removing the temporal term reduces accuracy by 8.18
points without collapsing effective rank.  Shuffling the class-persistent
order reduces accuracy by 7.55 points.  Temporal coherence therefore supplies
the task-aligned teaching signal in this controlled experiment.

The other two terms prevent different degeneracies.  Without gated variance
expansion, the median feature variance falls to $7.7\times10^{-5}$.  Without
decorrelation, feature variance remains high but effective rank falls to 2.8.
BatchNorm alone prevents neither failure.  These interventions assign distinct
empirical functions to the temporal, variance, and decorrelation terms, as
predicted by the soft-SFA formulation.

\subsection{Depth, readout, and representation geometry}

The final-layer probe attains $97.14\pm0.10$\%, 0.16 points below the matched
all-layer probe.  The paired interval $[-0.37,0.05]$ includes zero.  The final

accuracy is not obtained solely by concatenating a strong first layer with a
weak second layer.

After ten joint-training presentations, \TeReL{}-S attains
$96.29\pm0.07$\%.  It exceeds its unnormalized random reference by 1.16

with a soft-SFA tradeoff: the representation exposes persistent factors without
compressing every output direction into the same class geometry.

\subsection{Natural temporal order on PAMAP2}

The PAMAP2 comparison changes only the order presented to the encoder.
Architecture, initialization, optimizer, sample count, and probe remain fixed,
and activity classes are absent from encoder batches.  Chronological order
attains macro-F1 0.307; shuffled order attains 0.312.  Their paired interval,
$[-0.063,0.053]$, includes advantages in either direction.  Every method also
shows a large shift from validation subject 7 to test subject 8.  This single
heterogeneous split therefore resolves neither an ordered nor a shuffled
advantage.  Testing natural-order transfer requires several validation subjects
and tighter control of subject shift.

\subsection{Streaming execution and cost}

Validation selection for the samplewise configuration varies the learning
rate, state timescale, streaming normalizer, gradient accumulation, and number
of data presentations.  The selected two-pass configuration attains
$95.14\pm0.04$\% validation accuracy over three seeds.  Its effective rank is
112.8, with seed range 106.6--120.9, and its median feature variance is 0.378.
It exceeds the matched no-normalization random validation mean by 0.29 points
without retaining a temporal graph.

At each step, the preceding activation, running moments, normalization state,
and lateral matrix are detached.  The state size is independent of stream
length.  The resource-measurement seed performs one optimizer update per
observation and requires 1,305 seconds.  This time reflects substantial launch
and update overhead in the stock GPU/PyTorch implementation.  The experiment
establishes that samplewise learning can preserve a useful representation with
bounded storage; it establishes neither throughput nor energy efficiency.  The
resource measurements for the main methods appear in
\cref{tab:encoder-resources,tab:samplewise-streaming}.

\section{Limitations}

The method assumes that temporal neighbors share a latent cause.  Rapidly
changing or aliased causes can violate this assumption and make adjacency
misleading.  The MNIST ordering is synthetic and label-derived, so its accuracy
measures learning from a constructed temporal signal rather than label-free
learning.  PAMAP2 is a small sensor benchmark with substantial subject shift,
and one subject-disjoint split cannot resolve the broader natural-order
question.  Images, video, audio, and other natural streams must be evaluated
directly.  Any deployment on human activity data would also require
consent, privacy protection, and analysis of surveillance risk.

Bounded temporal history does not make the method cheap in layer width.  The
dense lateral operator requires $O(D^2)$ state and within-layer communication.
Sparse and low-rank alternatives require direct tests of representation
quality.  The samplewise result is a validation experiment on MNIST, not a
five-seed test evaluation or a throughput-matched comparison.  Its one-sample
optimizer updates are slow in the present GPU implementation.  Canonical
\TeReL{} likewise takes more wall-clock time than backpropagation despite its
lower declared operation proxy.  The software measurements support no claim of
hardware or energy efficiency.

BatchNorm in canonical \TeReL{} communicates across examples at each neuron,
and its affine parameters are learned.  Neuron-locality is therefore a spatial
claim, not a claim that the primary update is per-example.  The simpler
outer-product factorization applies only to variants with the identity
normalizer.  More generally, the locality claim concerns the path of credit
assignment.  The implementation still relies on standard optimizers, exact
activation derivatives, normalization, and synchronized matrix operations.

\section{Perspective and future directions}

\paragraph{Natural order.}
SFA is motivated by streams in which stable causes generate changing
observations, not by class-sorted images.  PAMAP2 provides no evidence that
adjacency helps under severe subject shift.  More informative settings include
video with controlled viewpoint change, egocentric interaction, and continuous
audio.  In these settings, the timescale of the latent cause can be compared
directly with the state and chunk timescales of \TeReL{}.  The next experiment
should vary these timescales rather than summarize natural order by a single
chronological-versus-shuffled contrast.

\paragraph{Constrained hardware.}
With identity normalization, each update requires the activity before the
synapse, a postsynaptic teaching factor, leaky state, and no error path across depth.  This
dependency structure is compatible with neuron-local or event-driven execution.
\TeReL{}-S also removes the temporal graph, and the samplewise experiment shows
that this stricter form can retain useful accuracy.  Compatibility does not
imply energy efficiency.  A hardware study would need low-rank lateral state,
fused state updates, and an optimizer designed for local arrays.

\paragraph{Recurrent extension.}
The temporal objective suggests a recurrent module whose slow representation
supports prediction without receiving gradients from the prediction path.  A
valid test must isolate the representation and prediction pathways, include
matched random-state and backpropagation-through-time controls, and repeat the
comparison across seeds.  This recurrent construction is a hypothesis implied
by the mechanism, not a result of the present study.

\section{Conclusion}

This work asks whether the SFA principle can train a deep nonlinear encoder
without propagating errors across layers.  \TeReL{} answers by assigning each
layer a temporal-coherence term, a gated variance-expansion term, and a
same-layer decorrelation signal.  Canonical \TeReL{} preserves short temporal
gradients within a chunk while blocking gradients across depth.  \TeReL{}-S
also detaches the temporal reference and retains state that is independent of
stream length, although its dense lateral matrix remains quadratic in width.

Under the controlled MNIST ordering, canonical \TeReL{} attains 97.30\%, 1.04
points below data-presentation-matched backpropagation and 1.95 points above the
BatchNorm-calibrated random encoder.  The final layer alone attains 97.14\%.
\TeReL{}-S attains 96.29\%, and its samplewise configuration attains 95.14\%
validation accuracy with effective rank 112.8.  Local SupCon attains 96.98\%,
0.32 points below \TeReL{} over the paired seeds.  The mechanism controls
separate the roles of the three signals: temporal coherence carries the
task-aligned relation, variance expansion prevents scale collapse, and
decorrelation prevents rank collapse.

These results show that temporal coherence, when protected against collapse
and redundancy, can train a two-hidden-layer representation with neuron-local
credit assignment.  This claim is confined to a constructed, label-derived
temporal relation.  The PAMAP2 comparison does not show that the
same advantage transfers to natural streams.  That question requires broader
experiments that control distribution shift and relate the timescale of the
data to the timescale of the learning rule.

\section{Gradient derivations}\label{app:gradients}

For a valid pair with a detached temporal reference,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_t}
\frac{1}{N_ID}\sum_s I_s\|z_s-\stopgrad(z_{s-1})\|^2
=\frac{2I_t}{N_ID}(z_t-p_t).
\end{equation}
Because $g$, $m$, $A$, and $q_t$ are detached,
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial L_V}{\partial z_t}
&=-\frac{2}{BD}g\odot(z_t-m),\\
\frac{\partial L_C}{\partial z_t}
&=\frac{1}{BD}Aq_t.
\end{align}
Adding these derivatives yields \cref{eq:local-gradient}.  The chain rule
through $\phi$ and then through the minibatch map $N_{\eta,\mathcal B}$ yields
the Jacobian-block expression in \cref{eq:weight-gradient}.  Setting $N$ to the
identity recovers the presynaptic outer-product special case.  With an
undetached temporal reference, $z_t$ appears as the current element of pair
$t$ and as the preceding element of pair $t+1$.  The sum of these two
derivatives gives \cref{eq:batch-gradient}.  Finite-fixture regression tests
verify the two signs and the future-activation term.

For the direct covariance control, let $Z_c$ stack centered rows and
$C=\offdiag(Z_c^\top Z_c/B)$.  Since $C$ is symmetric,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_c}\frac{\|C\|_F^2}{D}
=\frac{4}{BD}Z_cC,
\end{equation}
which is $4C(z_t-m)/(BD)$ in column-vector notation.  \TeReL{} replaces
$4C(z_t-m)$ by the detached direction $Aq_t$ and absorbs the positive factor
into $\lambda_C$.

\section{Implementation-fidelity tests}\label{app:tests}

The public test suite verifies the following properties:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the sign and gate of the variance gradient, detachment of the temporal
  reference, the future term for undetached time, the lateral-proxy error bound,
  and the direct-covariance derivative;
  \item nonzero updates to every declared encoder layer and lateral state, and
  the absence of a gradient from a later layer to an earlier layer;
  \item fixed dynamic-state size, bounded streaming normalization, release of
  samplewise gradients, and the absence of retained autograd graphs;
  \item training-split-only BatchNorm calibration that changes running buffers
  while preserving every random-encoder weight, bias, and affine parameter;
  \item the MNIST split roles, PAMAP2 subject roles, normalization fit only on
  the training split, temporal boundaries, and encoder batches containing only features and boundary
  batches;
  \item matched all-layer and final-layer probes, greedy layerwise scheduling,
  execution of every declared backpropagation layer, and exact counting of data
  presentations;
  \item batch-SFA constraints, boundary-aware derivatives, and numerical
  agreement of the published-algorithm IncSFA port with its reference; and
  \item rejection of held-out evaluation after any change to the protocol,
  selection ledger, configuration, or tracked source.
\end{itemize}
