\publicationabstract{%
We introduce Temporal Regularized Learning (\TeReL{}), a spatially and
temporally local learning rule for self-supervised representation learning
from sequential input streams. The key idea is to instantiate a VICReg-style
objective with temporally adjacent positives and non-differentiated running
population statistics, yielding a per-neuron online loss with bounded state.
The objective is a soft form of Slow Feature Analysis, and its gradient takes
the form of Hebbian feedforward and anti-Hebbian lateral updates, so that no
error signal crosses layers or time steps. On a class-chunked MNIST stream, a
two-layer fully connected network trained by \TeReL{} with batch size one
outperforms random features ($\FinalTeReLAccuracy$\% versus $95.09$\%
linear-probe accuracy) and classical SFA, and a variant that relaxes both
locality constraints is comparable to supervised backpropagation. The same
unlabeled updates can continue during inference without loss of accuracy.}

\section{Introduction}

Learning from unlabeled sensory streams is a practical necessity for both
biological modeling and energy-efficient machine learning on constrained
hardware. Modern self-supervised methods succeed on unlabeled data by aligning
representations of related samples (positives) while avoiding collapse using
either negative samples or explicit regularizers. Contrastive methods rely on
negatives and on large batch sizes to estimate cross-sample
statistics~\cite{chen2020,oord2018}. Regularized alternatives avoid explicit
negatives and instead combine an invariance term with variance and covariance
penalties~\cite{bardes2022,zbontar2021}. VICReg is a regularized method that
enforces invariance without negatives and whose regularizers can be computed
from local moments, at least in a batched implementation~\cite{bardes2022}.

Standard implementations of these objectives use credit assignment across
neurons and layers. That usually requires an extensive memory of the state for
backpropagation~\cite{rumelhart1986}, or iterative settling for predictive
coding and equilibrium propagation~\cite{rao1999,scellier2017}, which conflicts
with temporal locality. For this reason, we optimize the objective at each
neuron of the network. This paper shows how a VICReg-style objective can be
instantiated as a per-neuron temporal loss by using adjacent observations as
positives and treating running population statistics as non-differentiated
state. The motivation is practical: locality reduces communication between
units, and temporal locality permits streaming implementations with only a
small short-term memory per neuron. Both properties are desirable for analog
and neuromorphic hardware~\cite{indiveri2015,davies2018}.

By making the invariance temporal, \TeReL{} is a type of Slow Feature Analysis
(SFA) procedure: SFA aims to extract slowly changing features from a temporal
sequence of faster-changing inputs~\cite{wiskott2002,foldiak1991}. This can
lead to representations that are invariant to augmentations or capture latent
variables. More broadly, many sequential domains contain latent factors that
evolve more slowly than their raw observations. In text, the sentiment of a
review remains stable across its many words; in finance, stock prices
fluctuate faster than the market factors that drive them; in physiology, the
underlying state or health condition of a patient evolves slowly relative to
heartbeat, respiratory cycles, and momentary noise in sensor traces.

The per-neuron loss gives the learning rule a familiar form. Its gradient at
the preactivation of a neuron is a signed state; the feedforward update is the
product of presynaptic activity and this state, and pairs of states drive an
anti-Hebbian update of one same-layer matrix. Similarity-matching networks
use the same form of Hebbian cross-layer and anti-Hebbian lateral learning for
linear representations~\cite{pehlevan2015,pehlevan2018}. The key difference is
the more complex state used here: the residual of a nonlinear target that
balances slowness, variance, and decorrelation.

\paragraph{Contributions}
This paper makes three contributions. First, it derives \TeReL{} from VICReg
through an explicit instantiation choice: population statistics are maintained
as non-differentiated state, while adjacent observations supply the positive
pairs, and these stop-gradients retain the learning signal of the current
observation. Second, it expresses the resulting gradient as a neuron-local
rule with Hebbian feedforward and anti-Hebbian lateral updates, and clarifies
\TeReL{} as a conceptual bridge between modern regularized self-supervised
learning objectives and classical temporal representation learning ideas such
as Slow Feature Analysis and trace learning~\cite{foldiak1991,oja1982}. Third,
it evaluates \TeReL{} with batch size one under controlled temporal
orderings against random, classical SFA, supervised, and
relaxed-locality references, and reports ablations of each loss term.

The experiments test three hypotheses. (H1) Despite its locality constraints,
\TeReL{} trained with batch size one learns a useful deep representation.
(H2) The temporal order and each of the three loss terms contribute to this
representation. (H3) Continuing the same unlabeled updates along an evaluation
stream preserves the representation, so that the rule can keep learning
online.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Locality}

We call a learning rule \emph{spatially local} if the update of a weight
depends only on quantities available at its pre- and postsynaptic units (and
same-layer auxiliary units) and does not require backpropagated error signals
from other layers. We call it \emph{temporally local} (online) if the gradient
at time step $t$ depends only on the current observation, the current
parameters, and a bounded amount of detached state carried forward in time.
The size of this state may depend on the layer width but not on the length of
the stream, and no gradient flows through the history that produced it.

\subsection{Starting point: VICReg}

Consider observation $t$ at a layer with input $x_t\in\mathbb R^d$ and width
$D$. The layer computes
\begin{equation}
  a_t=W_tx_t+q_t,
  \qquad z_t=\phi(a_t),
  \label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
where $W_t\in\mathbb R^{D\times d}$ and $q_t\in\mathbb R^D$. The output $z_t$
is detached before it becomes the input to the next learned layer. Before this
observation, each neuron stores its previous activation $p_t$, a running mean
$\mu_t$, and a running variance $v_t$, and the layer stores a lateral matrix
$M_t\in\mathbb R^{D\times D}$. Let $I_t\in\{0,1\}$ indicate whether $p_t$
belongs to the same sequence, and write $\bar z_t=z_t-\mu_t$ and
$g_t=[\tau-v_t]_+$ for a variance target $\tau>0$.

VICReg decomposes a representation-level objective into three terms
(similarity/invariance, variance, covariance)~\cite{bardes2022}:
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{VICReg}}=\lambda_I\mathcal L_I+
\lambda_V\mathcal L_V+\lambda_C\mathcal L_C$. We keep these roles but replace
the second view by the previous activation $p_t$, batch moments by the
detached running statistics $\mu_t,v_t$, and the differentiated batch
covariance by the lagged operator $A_t=\offdiag(M_t)$:
\begin{equation}
\ell_T=\frac{I_t}{D}\lVert z_t-p_t\rVert_2^2,
\qquad
\ell_V=-\frac1D g_t^\top(\bar z_t\odot\bar z_t),
\qquad
\ell_C=\frac1D\bar z_t^\top A_t\stopgrad(\bar z_t).
\label{eq:local-losses}
\end{equation}
The layer minimizes $\ell=\lambda_T\ell_T+\lambda_V\ell_V+\lambda_C\ell_C$
with $\lambda_T>0$. The temporal coherence term makes consecutive activations
similar; in discrete time it is the squared first derivative of the
activations that SFA minimizes. The variance term increases the coordinates
whose stored variance lies below $\tau$, and the covariance term applies a
lagged same-layer decorrelation signal. \TeReL{} is therefore a causal
surrogate of the VICReg decomposition, not a re-expression of its batch
objective: $\ell_V$ is a gated instantaneous expansion term, and $A_t$ is a
lagged proxy for the covariance gradient.

\begin{proposition}[A stop-gradient VICReg instantiation is temporally local]
\label{prop:causal-vicreg}
For fixed $p_t,\mu_t,v_t,M_t$, and $I_t$, the derivative of $\ell$ is
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{z_t}\ell=\frac{2\lambda_T}{D}r_t,
  \qquad
  r_t=I_t(z_t-p_t)-\frac{\lambda_V}{\lambda_T}g_t\odot\bar z_t
       +\frac{\lambda_C}{2\lambda_T}A_t\bar z_t,
  \label{eq:causal-vicreg-gradient}
\end{equation}
and the feedforward gradient is $2\lambda_T(J_{\phi,t}^{\top}r_t)x_t^\top/D$,
where $J_{\phi,t}$ is the Jacobian of $\phi$ at $a_t$. The update is
temporally local, and detachment preserves a learning signal from the current
observation whenever $x_t\neq0$ and $J_{\phi,t}^{\top}r_t\neq0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The stored quantities are constants for differentiation. The temporal,
variance, and covariance terms contribute
$2\lambda_T I_t(z_t-p_t)/D$, $-2\lambda_V g_t\odot\bar z_t/D$, and
$\lambda_C A_t\bar z_t/D$, respectively, which sum to
\cref{eq:causal-vicreg-gradient}. All of them are functions of $z_t$ and of
quantities of size $O(D^2)$ computed before observation $t$, so no gradient
reaches an earlier observation. The chain rule through \cref{eq:forward}
gives the outer product $J_{\phi,t}^{\top}\nabla_{z_t}\ell\,x_t^\top$, which is
nonzero when both of its factors are nonzero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Regularized target and neuron state}

The residual $r_t$ induces an activation target,
$\widehat z_t=\stopgrad(z_t-r_t)$: updates move $z_t$ toward the previous
activation, away from the running mean where the stored variance is below
$\tau$, and against correlated activity. The local loss
$\lambda_T\lVert z_t-\widehat z_t\rVert_2^2/D$ has the same gradient with
respect to $z_t$ as $\ell$. This target is formed within the layer; it is not
an external target.

Expressed at the input side of the nonlinearity, the residual becomes
$b_t=J_{\phi,t}^{\top}r_t$. For the ReLU encoders used in the experiments,
\begin{equation}
  b_t=r_t\odot\mathbf 1[a_t>0],
  \label{eq:relu-state}
\end{equation}
which requires no automatic-differentiation graph. One pass through the lateral
matrix gives the postsynaptic neuron state
\begin{equation}
  s_t=b_t-\eta\kappa M_tb_t,
  \label{eq:state-correction}
\end{equation}
with correction step $\eta>0$ and strength $\kappa\geq0$. This is a single
matrix--vector product, not an iterative settling procedure.

As in the general case, the weight update can be implemented in a Hebbian
style, as the product of the input and an output-related term. With learning
rate $\alpha$ and weight decay $\lambda_W$,
\begin{equation}
  W_{t+1}=(1-\alpha\lambda_W)W_t
  -\alpha\frac{2\lambda_T}{D}s_tx_t^\top,
  \label{eq:weight-update}
\end{equation}
which is the gradient step for the detached preactivation target
$\widehat a_t=\stopgrad(a_t-s_t)$. Each feedforward synapse therefore receives
a presynaptic scalar $x_{t,i}$ and a postsynaptic scalar $s_{t,j}$, and under
gradient descent the sign of $s_{t,j}$ determines potentiation or depression.
The bias update uses $s_t$ alone. Without the lateral correction
($\kappa=0$), \cref{eq:weight-update} is exactly the feedforward gradient of
\cref{prop:causal-vicreg}.

\subsection{Lateral matrix and online memory requirements}

The same matrix supplies the covariance signal in
\cref{eq:causal-vicreg-gradient} and the state correction in
\cref{eq:state-correction}. After the synaptic update, it follows
\begin{equation}
  M_{t+1}=\rho M_t+(1-\rho)\big[(1-\omega)\offdiag(\bar z_t\bar z_t^\top)
      +\omega s_ts_t^\top\big],
  \label{eq:lateral-update}
\end{equation}
with $\omega\in[0,1]$ and $\rho\in[0,1)$. With Hebbian-style updates,
mean-centered products between pairs of neurons recover covariance over time.
If the inhibitory lateral weights are written as $L_t=-M_t$, the state
component contributes $-(1-\rho)\omega s_ts_t^\top$ to $L_t$: same-signed
neuron states make their mutual connection more inhibitory, which is the
anti-Hebbian sign.

The \TeReL{} loss requires three per-neuron state variables: the previous
activation, a running mean, and a running variance,
\begin{equation}
  p_{t+1}=z_t,
  \qquad
  \mu_{t+1}=\beta\mu_t+(1-\beta)z_t,
  \qquad
  v_{t+1}=\beta v_t+(1-\beta)(z_t-\mu_t)^2.
  \label{eq:causal-state}
\end{equation}
This state can be stored at the neuron level. The running statistics, the
lateral matrix \cref{eq:lateral-update}, and the feedforward weights
\cref{eq:weight-update} are all leaky integrators (exponential moving
averages); an RC circuit could, in principle, implement such leaky
integrators. At a sequence boundary only the validity of $p_t$ is cleared.
A layer of width $D$ thus stores $3D$ dynamic scalars and $D^2$ auxiliary
lateral parameters besides its feedforward weights; plain SGD adds no
optimizer state. For the $784$--$512$--$256$ MNIST encoder these are $2{,}304$
dynamic scalars, $327{,}680$ lateral parameters, and $533{,}248$ feedforward
parameters.

\TeReL{} does not attempt to approximate global backpropagation across long
paths in the network. Because the learning objective is local in space and
time, the method also does not accumulate approximation error with depth in
the way that methods approximating global gradients do. Each layer completes
its forward pass, target, state correction, and updates for observation $t$ in
one feedforward schedule.

\subsection{\TeReLOffline{}}

\TeReLOffline{} relaxes the temporal and spatial locality constraints within a
subsequence of the data stream. A subsequence enters a ReLU multilayer
perceptron, the same three-term objective is evaluated at its final layer, and
backpropagation differentiates it through all layers and observations of the
subsequence. It is optimized with plain SGD and serves as a performance
reference for the objective, not as an alternative definition of \TeReL{}.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\TeReL{} occupies a point in design space at the intersection of
self-supervised and local learning procedures. We summarize the most direct
comparisons as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Contrastive SSL:} Compared to methods that rely on negative
  samples, \TeReL{} requires only temporally adjacent positives and
  regularizers to avoid collapse~\cite{chen2020,oord2018}.
  \item \textbf{VICReg and Barlow Twins:} \TeReL{} moves the variance and
  covariance regularization to the neuron level and substitutes temporally
  adjacent positives for augmented pairs~\cite{bardes2022,zbontar2021}.
  \item \textbf{Layerwise SSL:} Layer-local and blockwise objectives remove
  the backward path between network modules~\cite{lowe2019,xiong2020,illing2021,siddiqui2024},
  and VICReg losses have been applied at successive
  layers~\cite{datta2025}. \TeReL{} differs by additionally targeting temporal
  locality and online implementability: it supplies the three signals causally
  along time and factors them into neuron-local updates.
  \item \textbf{Similarity matching:} Hebbian/anti-Hebbian networks derived
  from similarity-matching objectives have the same synaptic
  form~\cite{pehlevan2015,pehlevan2018}; \TeReL{} replaces their linear
  postsynaptic factor by the residual of a nonlinear regularized target.
  \item \textbf{Forward-Forward:} \TeReL{} and Forward-Forward both use local,
  layerwise objectives to avoid end-to-end backpropagation through deep
  networks. \TeReL{} uses an invariance objective rather than a goodness
  signal and does not require constructed positive and negative
  passes~\cite{hinton2022}.
  \item \textbf{Equilibrium propagation and predictive coding:} These rely on
  iterative relaxation, and equilibrium propagation on a free and a nudged
  phase, whereas \TeReL{} uses a single-pass, per-neuron objective with
  bounded local state~\cite{scellier2017,rao1999,lecun2006}.
\end{itemize}
Vectors and matrices that are computed from parameters or activations but held
constant with respect to the parameters being optimized are common in training
objectives, such as weight-averaged teacher parameters~\cite{tarvainen2017},
stop-gradient targets~\cite{grill2020,chenhe2021}, predictions of an earlier
model~\cite{li2016}, and importance matrices in continual
learning~\cite{kirkpatrick2017,wiest2026iewc}. \TeReL{} relies on this design
pattern through its running variance and covariance estimates.

\paragraph{Relation to slow feature analysis and trace learning}
\TeReL{} can be seen as a regularized, modernized trace-learning/SFA-style
procedure in which temporal adjacency provides the positive pairs. The MSE
between two contiguous samples is exactly the first derivative of activations
in discrete time that is squared and minimized in SFA, and the whitening
constraint of SFA maps to the variance and covariance
regularizers~\cite{wiskott2002}. In the classification experiments, the
faster-changing inputs are the individual samples, while the slower feature is
the class membership of all samples within a chunk. Incremental SFA adapts the
linear construction to streams but retains a whitening stage and linear
feature extraction~\cite{kompella2012}. \TeReL{} is also closely related to
Bio-SFA, a biologically plausible SFA algorithm. Bio-SFA works with a hard
whitening constraint and uses iterative settling, whereas \TeReL{} penalizes
covariance softly and its training is a series of discrete-time
passes~\cite{lipshutz2020}.

Trace learning is a simple, biologically plausible procedure that modifies
Hebbian updates by introducing a temporal aspect: instead of a neuron's
activation, it uses an activation trace~\cite{foldiak1991,stringer2002}.
Trace learning and \TeReL{} share the operational goal of promoting temporal
invariance but do so in different ways. Ignoring shared factors, the temporal
term of \TeReL{} induces $\Delta w_{kj}\propto x_k(z_{t-1,j}-z_{t,j})$, while
trace learning uses $\Delta w_{kj}\propto x_k(\beta\bar z_{t-1,j}+(1-\beta)z_{t,j})$
with $\beta\in(0,1)$. No choice of $\beta$ makes the two updates identical,
because matching them would require both $\beta=1$ and $1-\beta=-1$. The trace
rule furthermore requires a weight-dependent decay term to prevent runaway
effects~\cite{foldiak1990}.

More broadly, \TeReL{} opens a bridge between modern self-supervised learning
and classical local learning rules by showing how regularized objectives can
be expressed in strictly local, online-compatible form. Our ablations show
that the variance and decorrelation regularizers are both necessary to avoid
collapse or divergence. This suggests that trace-learning and SFA-style rules
may likewise benefit from explicit variance control and decorrelation, even if
their core temporal objective remains unchanged.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate \TeReL{} on MNIST~\cite{lecun1998} with
synthetic sequential coherence. \TeReL{}'s objective is strictly
representational and uses no supervision. However, the order of the data
stream can induce relatively arbitrary pairs of positives. For example, placing
data of the same category next to each other mirrors a supervised contrastive
setup. We use this order: the stream consists of randomly permuted
class-homogeneous chunks of 16 samples, separated by sequence boundaries. The
labels determine adjacency but do not enter the target or either update. As
noted by Hinton for the supervised Forward-Forward setup, the network should
then preferentially learn features that support discrimination between
classes~\cite{hinton2022}.

The encoder is a ReLU multilayer perceptron with hidden widths 512 and 256.
\TeReL{} is trained with batch size one and plain SGD for two passes over the
encoder-training samples. The downstream evaluation trains a linear classifier
on the standardized concatenated activations of both layers of the encoder-training samples
and evaluates it on held-out data. We reserve a stratified subset of the
official training data (10{,}000 images) for all
model selection; the official test split is used only for the final
comparison. Reported values are means and sample standard deviations over five
initializations, and paired comparisons use 95\% Student-$t$ intervals.
All trained models, including the linear classifier and the references, use
plain SGD; batch size is part of each method. Hyperparameters and raw values
appear in \cref{app:protocol}.

The references expose distinct relaxations. \TeReLOffline{} uses the same
architecture and objective with subsequences of 128 and end-to-end
backpropagation. A layer-local network trained with a supervised contrastive
objective~\cite{khosla2020} (Layer-local SupCon) uses labels, minibatches,
and batch normalization at every layer. A supervised offline network is
trained end to end with labels and backpropagation. A random ReLU encoder of the same shape, a
linear probe on the input, and batch and incremental SFA~\cite{wiskott2002,kompella2012}
complete the comparison. The methods differ in their intrinsic data and
optimization requirements, so the comparison reports representation quality
rather than equal compute.

Four ablations examine \TeReL{} itself: shuffling the stream, and setting the
temporal, variance, or covariance coefficient to zero. For the online
learning demonstration, the trained encoder continues its unlabeled updates
along the evaluation stream, using the same fitted probe as the fixed encoder.
Each representation is read before the current observation updates the
encoder. The learning rate of these continued updates is a fixed fraction of
the training learning rate, chosen on the validation stream.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The aim of the experiments is to show that \TeReL{} works in relatively small
networks under its locality constraints, not to compete with architectures
that rely on global credit assignment. As in the Forward-Forward
experiments~\cite{hinton2022}, we therefore use networks without weight
sharing. A shared weight would receive the sum of updates from many neurons,
so its update would no longer be local to one synapse.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/performance-mechanisms.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Representation quality and paired temporal effects on
  MNIST.} \textbf{a}, Held-out linear-probe accuracy (mean and sample standard
  deviation). \textbf{b}, Paired accuracy changes relative to \TeReL{} with a
  fixed encoder, with 95\% Student-$t$ intervals.}
  \label{fig:performance}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Representation quality (H1)}

\TeReL{} reaches $\FinalTeReLAccuracy\pm\FinalTeReLSD$\% held-out accuracy
on MNIST (\cref{fig:performance}a). The random encoder reaches
$95.09\pm0.23$\%, a paired gain of 1.39 percentage points $[1.04,1.74]$, and
the gain over a linear probe on the input is 3.94 points $[3.76,4.12]$. Batch
and incremental SFA reach $90.07\pm0.26$\% and $89.98\pm0.09$\%. Layer-local
SupCon reaches $\FinalSupConAccuracy\pm\FinalSupConSD$\%, 0.48 points $[0.10,0.85]$ above
\TeReL{}, with labels and minibatches at every layer. \TeReLOffline{} reaches
$\FinalOfflineAccuracy\pm\FinalOfflineSD$\%, on par with the fully supervised
offline network at $\FinalSupervisedAccuracy\pm\FinalSupervisedSD$\% (paired
difference $-0.03$ points $[-0.19,0.13]$). The
objective itself therefore supports supervised-level accuracy when
cross-layer and cross-time optimization are available; the gap to \TeReL{} is
the cost of the locality constraints.

\subsection{Temporal order and loss terms (H2)}

Removing the temporal order or any single loss term degrades the
representation (\cref{fig:performance}b). Shuffling the stream lowers accuracy by 1.08 paired
points $[0.75,1.40]$, and removing the temporal term lowers it by 1.50 points
$[1.33,1.67]$. Without the variance term, the representation collapses: the
median feature variance falls to about $1.4\times10^{-5}$ and accuracy to
$15.36\pm0.27$\%. Without the covariance term, training diverges for all five
initializations before a representation can be measured. As in
VICReg~\cite{bardes2022}, the invariance term alone admits trivial solutions;
the variance term prevents a constant representation, and the covariance term
keeps the multidimensional code stable.

\subsection{Online learning demonstration (H3)}

Because training and inference use the same per-sample computation, the
encoder can keep learning along the evaluation stream. With the training step
size, these continued updates are too aggressive for an already trained
representation: on the validation stream, the loss grows across the stream and
the effective rank drops sharply. Scaling the step by $10^{-3}$ preserves
validation accuracy ($96.51$\% versus $96.48$\% for the fixed encoder) and
effective rank. With this
scale fixed, continued updates on the held-out stream reach
$96.50\pm0.15$\%, compared with $96.48\pm0.15$\% for the fixed encoder, a
paired change of $+0.02$ points $[-0.01,0.04]$, while the feedforward weights
keep changing ($\lVert\Delta W\rVert_2=0.047$).

\subsection{Representations}

\Cref{fig:state} shows \TeReL{}'s neuron state $s_t$, the quantity that multiplies

alternate between positive and negative values, so the state drives both
potentiation and depression. Their scales differ substantially: some neurons
respond through sparse excursions, whereas another carries a broader signed
signal.

\section{Limitations and future work}

The experiments use a constructed, label-ordered stream on MNIST. Natural streams contain several time scales and may
require a different balance among the temporal, variance, and covariance
terms; establishing \TeReL{} on video or sensor streams is the most important
empirical extension. Continued online updates require a smaller step than
initial training, and in the present setting they preserve rather than
improve the representation; local rules that adapt this step would let
training and long-term inference coincide more fully. Finally, a layer of
width $D$ uses $D^2$ lateral parameters, which may dominate very wide layers.
Sparse, low-rank, or structured lateral matrices, as well as convolutional and
recurrent architectures, are natural next steps for constrained hardware.

\section{Conclusion and outlook}

We presented Temporal Regularized Learning, an approach that adapts the
invariance, variance, and covariance regularizers of VICReg to neuron-level
temporal losses. Detached running statistics and a lagged lateral matrix make
the rule local in space and time, and its gradient takes the form of Hebbian
feedforward and anti-Hebbian lateral updates driven by a signed neuron state.
Trained with batch size one, \TeReL{} learns representations that improve on
random features and classical SFA, and the same rule keeps
learning online after training. \TeReL{} emphasizes locality and streaming
compatibility and therefore maps naturally to constrained hardware, while
connecting regularized self-supervised learning to classical temporal ideas
such as Slow Feature Analysis and trace learning.
