\begin{abstract}
Temporal coherence is an attractive learning signal when
credit assignment must remain within a layer, but slowness alone admits a
collapsed solution.  We introduce Temporal Regularized Learning (\TeReL), a
deep local rule that combines temporal coherence, gated variance expansion, and
a same-layer decorrelation signal.  The objective is a soft neural form of Slow
Feature Analysis (SFA), viewed through the
variance--invariance--covariance structure of modern regularized
self-supervision.  We define two roles in the method family: canonical
\TeReL{} is greedy and layer-local while retaining gradients between adjacent
activations inside a short chunk; \TeReL{}-S detaches the temporal reference
and supports bounded-state samplewise execution.  We derive both gradients,
specify update order and boundary handling, and account separately for
parameters, optimizer state, dynamic state, and batch storage. All headline
experiments are rerun from an optimizer-corrected implementation
under a validation-selected, manifest-locked protocol.  On label-ordered
MNIST, canonical \TeReL{} reaches $97.30\pm0.07$\% test accuracy across five
seeds, versus $98.34\pm0.08$\% for data-presentation-matched backpropagation and
$95.35\pm0.19$\% for a normalization-matched random encoder.  Restricting the readout to the
last layer retains $97.14\pm0.10$\%, and \TeReL{}-S reaches
$96.29\pm0.07$\%.  A matched label-aware Local SupCon learner reaches
$96.98\pm0.10$\%; the paired \TeReL{} advantage is 0.32 points.  Validation
controls isolate the mechanism: removing
temporal coherence loses 8.18 points, removing variance expansion collapses
feature scale, and removing decorrelation collapses effective rank; the lagged
lateral direction has 0.76 mean cosine alignment with its same-batch target.  A
separately tuned, strictly samplewise \TeReL{}-S
configuration reaches $95.14\pm0.04$\% validation accuracy with no retained
temporal graph and fixed state.  A chronological PAMAP2 stress test remains
inconclusive and is reported as a secondary boundary, not the paper's center.
These results restore the competitive empirical finding in the constructed
MNIST regime while making precise which locality, streaming, and natural-order
conclusions the evidence supports.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Many observations change faster than the latent causes that generate them.
Object identity persists while viewpoint and illumination vary; a person's
activity persists across noisy wearable-sensor readings; and the state of a
dynamical system generally evolves more slowly than its measurement noise.
Temporal proximity can therefore replace hand-designed paired views as a
learning signal.  The difficulty is that making adjacent representations equal
also makes a constant representation optimal.

Classical Slow Feature Analysis (SFA) resolves this ambiguity by minimizing
temporal derivatives subject to zero-mean, unit-variance, and decorrelation
constraints~\cite{wiskott2002}.  Modern regularized self-supervised objectives
arrive at a closely related structure: an invariance term is paired with
variance and covariance regularizers to prevent collapse~\cite{bardes2022,
zbontar2021}.  The shared structure is not itself new.  Our question is instead
an implementation question: can this soft-SFA objective train each nonlinear
layer without gradients from later layers or through temporal history?

\TeReL{} answers with three same-layer signals.  A temporal term attracts the
current activation to its preceding activation.  A neuron-local term
expands activity while its detached running variance lies below a target.  A
dense same-layer operator tracks off-diagonal second moments and supplies a
detached approximation to the covariance-penalty gradient.  Detaching the
input to every subsequent layer makes the parameter update spatially local in
the credit-assignment sense.  Canonical \TeReL{} retains the preceding
activation in a short within-chunk graph and is therefore layer-local but not
strictly online.  \TeReL{}-S detaches that reference, making the rule causal
and bounded-history.  Minibatches may still execute either variant efficiently;
batching is not itself the locality claim.

This distinction matters because three claims are easily conflated.  First,
local credit assignment does not imply sparse communication: the lateral
operator communicates within a layer.  Second, bounded history does not imply
constant state in width: a dense width-$D$ lateral operator costs $D^2$.
Third, an objective without label terms is not self-supervised if labels were
used to construct its temporal order.  We therefore use label-ordered MNIST as
a controlled temporal-supervision study.  Chronological wearable streams with
activity classes withheld from the encoder loss provide a secondary natural-order
stress test; their inconclusive outcome is not allowed to displace the
controlled competitive result.

The paper makes five contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formulate \TeReL{} as a deep, layer-local soft-SFA rule and locate
  its contribution relative to SFA, Incremental SFA, Bio-SFA, VICReg, and
  gradient-isolated predictive, contrastive, and blockwise self-supervised learning.
  \item We give the implemented objective, exact activation and local parameter
  gradients for detached and undetached time, including the canonical
  within-layer normalization Jacobian, the detached state updates, and
  a quantitative error decomposition for the shifted lateral heuristic, and
  measure the successful lagged operator against its same-batch direction.
  \item We recover competitive constructed-MNIST performance from an optimizer-corrected
  implementation: canonical \TeReL{} lies 1.04 percentage points behind
  data-presentation-matched backpropagation and 1.95 points above a
  normalization-matched random encoder.
  A matched local supervised-contrastive comparator and a direct-covariance
  control delimit what this result attributes to the soft-SFA construction.
  \item We demonstrate tuned batch-size-one learning with fixed state and no
  temporal graph, and report parameters, optimizer state, dynamic state, peak
  memory, compute proxies, and throughput separately.
  \item We provide validation-only selection, matched architectures and
  readouts, five confirmatory seeds, raw results, uncertainty, and exact
  result-to-commit provenance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Temporal Regularized Learning}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{From constrained SFA to a soft local objective}

Let $x_t$ be a temporal stream and let layer $\ell$ produce, for local
minibatch $\mathcal B$,
\begin{equation}
  a_t^{\ell}=W^{\ell}z_t^{\ell-1}+c^{\ell},\qquad
  h_{\mathcal B}^{\ell}=N_{\eta^\ell,\mathcal B}(a_{\mathcal B}^{\ell}),\qquad
  z_t^{\ell}=\phi(h_t^{\ell}).
\end{equation}
Here $N$ is either the identity or the within-layer normalizer with affine
parameters $\eta^\ell$.  The canonical encoder uses training-mode BatchNorm,
so $N_{\eta,\mathcal B}$ couples examples inside the current local minibatch;
it does not introduce an error path from a later layer.
SFA searches for features that change slowly while excluding constant and
duplicated solutions.  In one common statement it minimizes
$\mathbb{E}\|z_t-z_{t-1}\|_2^2$ subject to zero mean, unit variance, and
decorrelated outputs~\cite{wiskott2002}.  \TeReL{} replaces hard population
constraints by detached running state and local soft terms.  This is a
particular neural parameterization and optimizer, not a new discovery of the
SFA objective.

For one layer, consider $B$ activations $z_t\in\mathbb{R}^D$.  A Boolean
boundary $b_t$ marks the start of a subject, recording, or constructed chunk.
Let $I_t=1-b_t$, additionally set to zero when no stored predecessor exists,
and let $N_I=\sum_t I_t$.  For minibatch $k$, the state available before the
step is the detached triple $(m_{k-1},v_{k-1},A_{k-1})$; we suppress its batch
index in the local objective:
\begin{align}
L_S &= \frac{1}{N_ID}\sum_{t:I_t=1}\|z_t-p_t\|_2^2,\label{eq:similarity}\\
L_V &= -\frac{1}{BD}\sum_{t=1}^{B}\sum_{j=1}^{D}
  [\gamma-v_j]_+(z_{tj}-m_j)^2,\label{eq:variance}\\
L_C &= \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{t=1}^{B}(z_t-m)^\top A q_t,\label{eq:covariance}\\
L &= \lambda_S L_S+\lambda_VL_V+\lambda_CL_C.\label{eq:terel}
\end{align}
If $N_I=0$, $L_S$ and its gradient are defined as zero.  In canonical
\TeReL{}, $p_t=z_{t-1}$ inside a short chunk and
$q_t=\stopgrad(z_t-m)$.  The input to each subsequent layer remains detached,
so these within-chunk temporal gradients never become gradients across depth.
In \TeReL{}-S, $p_t=\stopgrad(z_{t-1})$ (or the detached last activation from
the preceding call).  Boundary flags invalidate temporal pairs in both cases.

The minus sign in \cref{eq:variance} is essential.  With the gate
$g_j=[\gamma-v_j]_+$ treated as state, minimizing $L_V$ pushes a currently
active unit away from its mean until its running variance reaches $\gamma$.
The term is a local gradient surrogate for a variance constraint; it is not the
usual nonnegative VICReg scalar variance penalty.  Its effectiveness depends on
the statistics timescale and normalization.  We therefore treat state rates,
normalization, and activation choice as algorithmic hyperparameters and select
them on validation data.

\subsection{Lateral decorrelation as a gradient proxy}

Define the detached same-minibatch off-diagonal second-moment operator
\begin{equation}
C_k=\offdiag\left(\frac{1}{B}\sum_t(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})
(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})^\top\right).
\end{equation}
The direct control used in our ablations minimizes
$L_{\mathrm{direct}}=\|C_k\|_F^2/D$.  With $m_{k-1}$ detached, its activation
gradient is $4C_k(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})/(BD)$.  \TeReL{} instead uses the operator
$A_{k-1}$ available before the step and
\cref{eq:covariance}; because $q_t$ and $A$ are detached, the encoder receives
$A_{k-1}q_{k,t}/(BD)$.  If $A_{k-1}\approx C_k$ and
$q_{k,t}=z_{k,t}-m_{k-1}$, this matches the direct gradient direction up to a
positive factor four.  It is a lagged proxy for the gradient, not an equality
between scalar objectives.

The shifted heuristic uses the preceding centered activation as $q_t$.  Let
$u_t=z_t-m$.  Its error relative to the direct signal obeys
\begin{equation}
\|Aq_t-Cu_t\|_2
\leq \|A-C\|_2\|u_t\|_2+\|A\|_2\|q_t-u_t\|_2.
\label{eq:shift-bound}
\end{equation}
The two terms expose separate failure modes: an inaccurate lateral estimate
and a representation that changes too quickly for a one-step shift.  The bound
does not guarantee a useful shifted signal, so we report this variant only as
an ablation.

\subsection{Exact local gradients}\label{sec:gradients}

Write $g=[\gamma-v]_+$.  For \TeReL{}-S the loss gradient with respect to the
post-activation is
\begin{equation}
e_t=\frac{2\lambda_S I_t}{N_ID}(z_t-p_t)
-\frac{2\lambda_V}{BD}g\odot(z_t-m)
+\frac{\lambda_C}{BD}Aq_t.
\label{eq:local-gradient}
\end{equation}
Let $r_s=e_s\odot\phi'(h_s^\ell)$ and let
$J_{st}^{N}=\partial h_s^\ell/\partial a_t^\ell$ be the minibatch Jacobian
block of the within-layer normalizer.  The input $z_t^{\ell-1}$ to each learned
layer is detached.  The exact same-layer chain rule is therefore
\begin{equation}
u_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal B}(J_{st}^{N})^\top r_s,\qquad
\frac{\partial L^{\ell}}{\partial W^{\ell}}
=\sum_t u_t(z_t^{\ell-1})^\top,
\qquad
\frac{\partial L^{\ell}}{\partial c^{\ell}}
=\sum_t u_t,
\qquad
\frac{\partial L^{\ell}}{\partial\eta^\ell}
=\sum_s\left(\frac{\partial h_s^\ell}{\partial\eta^\ell}\right)^\top r_s.
\label{eq:weight-gradient}
\end{equation}
For $N=\mathrm{Id}$, $J_{st}^{N}=\delta_{st}I$ and the weight update reduces
to the presynaptic outer product
$\sum_t(e_t\odot\phi'(a_t^\ell))(z_t^{\ell-1})^\top$.  Training-mode
BatchNorm instead mixes the output-side factors across examples in
$\mathcal B$ and also updates $\eta^\ell$.  Autograd evaluates this local
Jacobian exactly.  In both cases no later-layer error enters
\cref{eq:weight-gradient}; the simple per-example factorization applies only
to the identity-normalizer case.

Canonical \TeReL{} leaves adjacent $p_t=z_{t-1}$ inside the short chunk graph.
Its temporal contribution is
\begin{equation}
\frac{2\lambda_S}{N_ID}
\left[I_t(z_t-z_{t-1})-I_{t+1}(z_{t+1}-z_t)\right],
\label{eq:batch-gradient}
\end{equation}
with missing and boundary-crossing terms omitted.  The second term depends on a
future activation within the chunk.  We therefore describe canonical \TeReL{}
as layer-local but not temporally online; \TeReL{}-S is both layer-local and
temporally local.  Across minibatch calls, even canonical \TeReL{} reads its
stored predecessor as detached state.  In the frozen MNIST construction, a
mean 151.2 of 46,866 valid edges per data presentation cross such a boundary
(0.32\%; 9,073 edges over the 60-pass encoder schedule); all remaining valid
edges use the live within-call gradient in \cref{eq:batch-gradient}.

\subsection{State update and execution order}

After the optimizer step for minibatch $k$, state is updated under
\texttt{no\_grad} from the activations computed for that step:
\begin{align}
m_k &= \rho_m m_{k-1}+(1-\rho_m)\bar z_k,\label{eq:mean-update}\\
v_k &= \rho_m v_{k-1}+(1-\rho_m)\frac1B\sum_t
(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})^2,\label{eq:var-update}\\
A_k &= \rho_A A_{k-1}+(1-\rho_A)\offdiag\left(
\frac1B\sum_t(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})(z_{k,t}-m_{k-1})^\top\right).
\label{eq:lat-update}
\end{align}
Thus $\rho_A=0$ makes $A_k=C_k$, but the loss for batch $k$ has already used
$A_{k-1}$; the successful configuration is a one-minibatch-lagged proxy.  The
pre-step mean $m_{k-1}$ is used in \cref{eq:var-update,eq:lat-update}.  For
\TeReL{}-S, the final activation and its centered value are stored as detached
temporal state.  No activation graph survives the call.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{One \TeReL{}-S step for a deep encoder.}
\label{alg:terel}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require input batch/stream segment $X$, boundary flags $b$, layer parameters
  $\{W^\ell,c^\ell,\eta^\ell\}$, and states
  $\{m^{\ell},v^{\ell},A^{\ell},p^{\ell}\}$
\State $h\gets X$
\For{layer $\ell=1,\ldots,L$}
  \State $z^{\ell}\gets\phi(N_{\eta^\ell,\mathcal B}(W^{\ell}\stopgrad(h)+c^{\ell}))$
  \State construct detached predecessors $p_t^{\ell}$ and valid pairs from $b$
  \State compute $L^{\ell}$ by \cref{eq:terel}
  \State $h\gets\stopgrad(z^{\ell})$
\EndFor
\State backpropagate $\sum_{\ell}L^{\ell}$ only within each layer, including
  $N_{\eta^\ell,\mathcal B}$; step optimizer
\For{layer $\ell=1,\ldots,L$}
  \State update detached state by \cref{eq:mean-update,eq:var-update,eq:lat-update}
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Our primary locality claim is spatial: parameters receive no gradient from
another layer.  Canonical \TeReL{} keeps short-range temporal credit within a
chunk; \TeReL{}-S additionally cuts credit across time and is online in the
bounded-history sense when executed at batch size one.  We do not equate these
properties with biological plausibility or measured hardware efficiency.

\subsection{State and memory cost}\label{sec:cost}

For layer width $D$, dynamic \TeReL{} state contains mean $D$, variance $D$,
lateral operator $D^2$, previous activation $D$, previous centered activation
$D$, and one validity flag: $D^2+4D+1$ elements.  A bounded streaming
normalizer, when used, adds location and scale vectors of size $2D$.  Both
totals are constant in stream length and quadratic in width.  Encoder
parameters, optimizer moments, batch
activations, and temporary matrix products are separate costs.  In particular,
AdamW adds approximately two parameter-sized moment buffers after its first
step.  The lateral state is discarded at inference, but its training cost is
real.  Batch-size-one execution removes batch growth and temporal graphs; it
does not remove the dense lateral matrix.

\section{Relation to prior work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{SFA and incremental SFA.}
SFA is the objective-level ancestor of \TeReL{}: temporal slowness, variance,
and decorrelation are its defining ingredients~\cite{wiskott2002}.  Batch
linear SFA whitens observations and solves a minor-component problem for their
derivatives.  IncSFA replaces those batch operations with covariance-free
incremental PCA and minor-component updates, supports episodic boundaries, and
has Hebbian/anti-Hebbian update forms~\cite{kompella2012}.  It is therefore a
stronger online precedent than a comparison to batch SFA alone.  Our empirical
baseline is a provenance-documented Python 3 port of the authors' published
linear algorithm, validated against batch SFA on a synthetic stream.  \TeReL{}
differs by applying a soft objective directly to each nonlinear learned layer,
at the cost of a dense tracked lateral operator.

PowerSFA instead makes approximate whitening differentiable and thereby trains
deep SFA models end to end~\cite{schuler2018}.  This establishes that a neural,
gradient-trained SFA parameterization is not itself the contribution here;
\TeReL{} removes the gradient path across learned layers and, in \TeReL{}-S,
across temporal history.  Deep temporal-coherence objectives also predate this
work: for example, Slow and Steady Feature Analysis regularizes first- and
higher-order changes across video tuples in a convolutional encoder
~\cite{jayaraman2016}.  \TeReL{} returns to first-order adjacency to study the
distinct question of layer-local soft constraints and bounded temporal state.

\paragraph{Bio-SFA and trace learning.}
Bio-SFA derives an online neural network for the SFA problem with local
synaptic updates~\cite{lipshutz2020}.  This prevents us from presenting local
online SFA as absent from prior work.  \TeReL{} instead contributes a different
deep soft-penalty parameterization and an explicit stop-gradient implementation
that is easy to compose with standard nonlinear layers.  F\"oldi\'ak-style
trace learning also exploits temporal persistence~\cite{foldiak1991}; its
positive activation trace is not algebraically the same as the signed
difference in \cref{eq:local-gradient}.

\paragraph{Gradient-isolated predictive and contrastive learning.}
Greedy InfoMax trains a stack of gradient-isolated modules with local InfoNCE
objectives that predict across the natural order of images, audio, and
video~\cite{lowe2019}.  It is therefore a direct precedent for combining
temporal structure with greedy depth-local representation learning.  CLAPP
makes contrastive prediction causal and local in both space and time through
pre/post activity, predictive dendritic input, and broadcast modulation, while
still relying on positive and negative examples~\cite{illing2021}.  LoCo instead
improves paired-view local contrastive learning with overlapping blocks, which
allows the next block's local loss to influence parameters in the overlap and
therefore relaxes strict gradient isolation~\cite{xiong2020}.  These methods
preclude claiming either local temporal prediction or gradient-isolated deep
learning as new here.  Relative to them, \TeReL{} studies a noncontrastive
soft-SFA loss with explicit variance and redundancy controls; \TeReL{}-S uses
no negative set or predictive decoder, but pays for a dense lagged same-layer
operator.  Our constructed MNIST protocol is also label-assisted through
ordering, unlike their principal unlabeled settings.

\paragraph{Regularized and blockwise self-supervision.}
VICReg and Barlow Twins use variance/covariance or cross-correlation penalties
to prevent collapse without negatives~\cite{bardes2022,zbontar2021}.  Blockwise
self-supervised learning has already shown that VICReg, Barlow Twins, and
contrastive losses can train network blocks without end-to-end
backpropagation~\cite{siddiqui2024}.  \TeReL{} is therefore not distinguished by
``a local VICReg loss'' alone.  Its narrower focus is temporal positives,
detached streaming moments, and a same-layer covariance-gradient proxy that can
execute one observation at a time.

Two boundaries sharpen that comparison.  Siddiqui et al. find simultaneous
block training materially stronger than sequential freezing, indicating that
forward-path co-adaptation remains useful even when gradients stop at block
boundaries~\cite{siddiqui2024}; the canonical \TeReL{} result instead uses
greedy layerwise training.  Datta et al. likewise apply VICReg losses at
individual layers, using original/augmented image pairs and current-batch
variance and covariance terms~\cite{datta2025}.  In contrast, \TeReL{} uses a
temporal relation, a gated detached variance state, and a lagged lateral
gradient proxy.  Spectral analyses of SSL further show that the chosen positive
relation can determine downstream geometry when it aligns with task
labels~\cite{balestriero2022}.  Consequently, our class-chunk MNIST result is a
study of constructed pair supervision and requires a matched label-aware local
baseline; it is not evidence that arbitrary natural adjacency reveals class
structure.

\section{Experimental protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

The experiments answer four questions.  First, after restoring the intended
training schedule and optimizer state, how does canonical \TeReL{} compare with
data-presentation-matched backpropagation, normalization-matched and
no-normalization random encoders, and a label-aware local learner under the
constructed supervision?  Second, is that
result carried only by early layers or retained by the final layer?  Third, can
\TeReL{}-S learn non-collapsed representations one sample at a time with bounded
state?  Fourth, as a secondary stress test, does the temporal-coherence
advantage transfer to a natural wearable stream?

\subsection{Data roles and leakage control}

\paragraph{MNIST controlled temporal supervision.}
The official 60,000-example training split is stratified with seed 1701 into
50,000 training and 10,000 validation examples.  The official 10,000-example
test split is excluded from v2 configuration recovery.  Within each
encoder epoch, training examples are randomly arranged into same-class chunks
of length 16; all chunk starts are temporal boundaries.  Labels create the
persistent order but do not enter the \TeReL{} loss.  This controlled setting
isolates whether temporal continuity can provide a useful local teaching
signal; it is not presented as unlabeled self-supervision.

\paragraph{PAMAP2 secondary natural-order stress test.}
PAMAP2 contains 100-Hz wearable measurements and activity annotations from
nine subjects~\cite{reiss2012pamap2}.  We use its 12 protocol activities,
discard transition label 0 as instructed by the dataset documentation, and
downsample by a fixed stride of 10.  Missing sensors are imputed with
training-subject means and standardized using training-subject statistics.
Subjects 1--6 train the encoder and probe, subject 7 is validation, and subject
8 is the untouched test subject.  Subject 9 is excluded before model selection:
its protocol recording contains only rope jumping and cannot support a 12-class
test.  Boundaries are inserted at subject starts, removed-label gaps, and
timestamp discontinuities.  The encoder iterator returns only features and
boundaries, but annotation label 0 determines which transition rows are
removed and where gap boundaries occur.  Activity-class labels therefore do
not enter the encoder loss, although annotations influence preprocessing.  This
experiment tests generalization of the temporal intuition; it does not replace
the controlled MNIST comparison.

\subsection{Models, baselines, and readouts}

The primary encoder is $784\rightarrow512\rightarrow256$ with LeakyReLU
(negative slope 0.01).  Canonical \TeReL{} uses affine batch normalization
(update momentum 0.1), live
temporal pairs within chunks, and greedy layerwise training for 30 epochs per
layer.  Thus both its encoder and the matched BP encoder receive 60 complete
training-set presentations.  Its AdamW learning rate is $3\times10^{-4}$,
weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 256, and coefficients
$(\lambda_S,\lambda_V,\lambda_C)=(1,2.5,1)$.  Population momentum is 0.9;
$\rho_A=0$ writes each minibatch operator after its optimizer step, so the next
minibatch uses that preceding-minibatch operator.

Backpropagation uses the same hidden dimensions, activation, normalization,
optimizer, batch size, and 60 data passes, trained end to end with cross
entropy.  \TeReL{}-S uses detached temporal references, joint local training,
no batch normalization, $(1,5,1)$, and a separately validated ten-pass
schedule.  It is an intentionally lean temporally local variant, so its compute
is reported rather than described as matched to BP.  The original random
reference retains the same seed-specific hidden weights but omits normalization;
we keep it because it matches the no-normalization \TeReL{}-S architecture.
The acceptance-critical random control instead includes the canonical affine
BatchNorm modules.  Its hidden weights and biases remain at initialization, as
do BatchNorm scale one and offset zero.  Under \texttt{no\_grad}, one pass over
the 50,000 training examples in stored order (batch size 256) calibrates only
running means and variances with update momentum 0.1 (0.9 retention).  The
model then enters evaluation mode, and those
fixed train-only statistics are exposed to the same all-layer probe and test
examples.  This is data-dependent normalization, not an encoder-parameter
update.

Canonical minibatches concatenate randomly permuted class-homogeneous chunks,
so each batch can mix classes while BatchNorm communicates across all 256 local
examples.  Under greedy training, only the active layer's BatchNorm is in
training mode: layer-one statistics are fixed before layer two is trained.
Representation extraction and probing use evaluation mode with the final
running statistics and learned affine parameters.  Thus BatchNorm changes the
within-layer information path but never carries error across depth.

Local SupCon supplies a label-aware local comparison for the constructed MNIST
regime.  Each layer receives a supervised-contrastive loss over every same-label
positive in its minibatch; activations are detached between layers, while both
layers update simultaneously.  It uses the same architecture, batch
normalization, batch size, 60 data presentations, all-layer probe, and test
seeds as the primary comparison.  A validation-only $3\times2$ grid varies
learning rate in $\{10^{-4},3\times10^{-4},10^{-3}\}$ and temperature in
$\{0.1,0.2\}$, the same number of candidates used for no \TeReL{} component or
headline-matrix selection.

The primary readout for every method is the same linear classifier over the
concatenation of both hidden layers.  A final-layer-only probe on the identical
canonical encoder is a secondary audit of depth specialization.  All probes use
AdamW for 60 epochs (learning rate 0.003, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size
2,048).  This matched-access design prevents a comparison in which a local
method exposes less of its representation than the baseline.

\subsection{Validation recovery and freeze}

Selection uses only the 10,000-example validation split and seeds 101, 202,
and 303.  We first reconstructed the original successful ingredients and then
varied one factor at a time: temporal detachment, joint versus greedy layer
training, lateral-state timescale, normalization, readout, augmentation, and
schedule.  The principal regression was not caused by the corrected loss: the
failed protocol had simultaneously shortened training from 60 to 10 passes,
changed greedy to joint optimization, detached time, removed normalization,
restricted the readout, and set $\rho_A=0.99$ in place of the validated
one-minibatch-lagged operator ($\rho_A=0$).
A bounded validation-only recovery restored those ingredients before the
confirmatory matrix was frozen.

The streaming configuration is selected separately because batch-size-one
execution changes both normalization and update count.  It uses \TeReL{}-S,
bounded running normalization, two passes, learning rate $6\times10^{-6}$,
statistics and lateral momenta 0.99, and one forward/backward step per example.
No future example, retained temporal graph, or stream-length-dependent buffer
is used.

\subsection{Confirmatory analysis and test gate}

Central results use seeds 1101, 1202, 1303, 1404, and 1505.  We report every
raw result, the mean, sample standard deviation, a small-sample Student-$t$
interval, and a percentile 95\% bootstrap interval from 10,000 resamples
(analysis seed 260803).  Primary contrasts pair methods by seed; Student-$t$
intervals are primary and bootstrap intervals are retained as a sensitivity
analysis.  The preregistered competitive gate places canonical \TeReL{} within
1.5 percentage points of BP; this was an operational absolute-accuracy target
chosen before v2, not a claim of error-rate parity.  The original v2 learned-
representation gate requires improvement over the no-normalization random
encoder; the later acceptance-critical audit repeats that test with canonical
BatchNorm included.
Non-collapse requires every canonical seed to have effective rank at least 20,
median variance at least 0.05, and active fraction at least 0.25.

Before the v2 test run, all implementation tests passed; the validation ledger,
resolved matrix, code commit, and protocol were hash-locked in a clean manifest;
and the runner required an explicit test flag.  The MNIST test set was not used
during v2 recovery, but it had been observed by the rejected submission and by
the earlier diagnostic revision.  We therefore treat v2 as a sequential
confirmation rather than calling the test split historically untouched.  No
v2 hyperparameter was changed after opening the gate.  The subsequent
mechanism audit was separately frozen and uses validation data only; it removes
one loss coefficient or shuffles order without selecting a replacement model.

The later review patch follows the same separation.  All six Local SupCon
candidates and both lateral controls are run on validation seeds 101, 202, and
303.  The selected comparator is then frozen in a one-row manifest before its
five test seeds are evaluated.  These additions do not alter, select among, or
rerun any v2 headline method.

The normalization audit is another one-row extension.  Its calibration
treatment and the same five test seeds were fixed a priori by the review rather
than selected by validation performance.  The manifest was frozen at a clean
execution commit before the test gate opened; exactly five random-encoder runs
were executed, and no v2 or v3 record was changed or rerun.

non-collapse criteria, and every intended layer and normalization group has a
nonzero parameter update.  The corrected evidence therefore supports the
paper's central empirical statement within this constructed MNIST regime: a
two-hidden-layer network trained by layer-local soft-SFA reaches 97.30\%.

\subsection{Mechanism isolation}

After the confirmatory matrix was complete, we froze a validation-only
one-factor audit under the recovered canonical protocol.  Removing the temporal
term loses 8.18 percentage points without collapsing rank, while fully

expansion, median feature variance falls to $7.7\times10^{-5}$; without
decorrelation, variance remains high but effective rank falls to 2.8.  Batch
normalization alone does not rescue either failure.  The three signals thus
have empirically distinct roles matching the soft-SFA interpretation.

\subsection{Natural temporal order on PAMAP2}

The chronological/shuffled PAMAP2 contrast was introduced to answer whether
the controlled temporal mechanism transfers when order is natural and labels
are excluded from encoder batches.  Architecture, initialization, optimizer,
sample count, and probe are held fixed; only encoder order changes.  The result
is inconclusive: chronological and shuffled learning reach 0.307 and 0.312
macro-F1, respectively, with a paired interval spanning both directions
($[-0.063,0.053]$).  All methods also exhibit a large subject-7 to subject-8
shift.  We report this boundary because it is informative, but do not treat a
single heterogeneous wearable split as a reversal of the controlled MNIST
result or of the temporal-coherence motivation.  Broader natural-stream tests
need multi-subject validation and stronger shift control.

\subsection{Streaming execution and cost}

The first untuned batch-size-one audit verified bounded state but produced weak
rank.  We treated that as a starting point rather than a conclusion and tested
learning rate, state timescale, streaming normalization, accumulation, and
exposure count on validation data.  The selected two-pass configuration reaches
$95.14\pm0.04$\% accuracy across three seeds, with effective rank
$112.8$ (seed range 106.6--120.9) and median feature variance 0.378.  It exceeds
the matched no-normalization random validation mean by 0.29 points while retaining no
temporal graph.

At every step, the previous activation, running moments, normalization state,
and lateral matrix are detached; state size is unchanged by stream length.  The
serial resource seed processes one observation per optimizer step and takes
1,305 seconds, illustrating that a stock GPU/PyTorch implementation pays a
large launch and update overhead.  This is evidence for quality-bearing
samplewise learning and bounded storage, not a claim that the present software
is throughput- or energy-optimal.  Encoder resource accounting for the main
methods is given in \cref{tab:corrected-resources,tab:streaming-v2}.

\section{Limitations}

\TeReL{} inherits the temporal-coherence assumption: rapidly changing or
aliased latent causes can make adjacent observations misleading.  The MNIST
ordering is synthetic and label-derived, so its high accuracy establishes the
efficacy of the local temporal teaching signal rather than label-free learning.
PAMAP2 is a modest, distribution-shifted sensor benchmark, and its single
subject-disjoint split cannot decide the broader natural-order question.
Scaling to images, video, audio, and additional natural streams remains an
empirical requirement.
Activity-monitoring deployments would also require consent, privacy, and
surveillance-risk analysis beyond this methodological benchmark.

The dense lateral operator gives bounded history but $O(D^2)$ state and
within-layer communication.  Sparse or low-rank approximations need direct
quality tests.  The streaming result is validation evidence on MNIST, not a
five-seed test-set or throughput-matched comparison; samplewise optimizer steps
are slow in the present GPU implementation.  Canonical \TeReL{} also takes
more wall-clock time than BP despite a lower declared operation proxy, so no
hardware or energy advantage follows from these software measurements.

Canonical BatchNorm is a same-layer, cross-example communication channel and
its affine parameters are learned.  Depth-local credit assignment therefore
does not imply a per-example local update for the headline model; the simpler
outer-product factorization applies to the identity-normalizer variants.

The five-seed v2 matrix was frozen after validation-only recovery, but the
official MNIST test set was historically seen by the rejected submission and
the separate diagnostic revision.  This is a sequential confirmation, not a
pristine first test access.  An independent benchmark should provide the next
strong confirmation.  Finally, locality here concerns credit assignment:
standard optimizers, exact activation derivatives, normalization, and
synchronized matrix operations remain.

\section{Perspective and future directions}

\paragraph{Natural order.}
SFA's motivating regime is not class-sorted images but streams in which stable
causes generate many changing observations.  The PAMAP2 result shows that
adjacency alone is insufficient under severe subject shift; it does not remove
the underlying opportunity.  Video under controlled viewpoint change,
egocentric interaction, and continuous audio provide cleaner tests in which
the timescale of the latent cause can be compared directly with the \TeReL{}
state and chunk timescales.  A useful next study should vary those timescales
rather than reduce natural order to one chronological-versus-shuffled number.

\paragraph{Constrained hardware.}
For the identity-normalizer and streaming forms, the rule's
ingredients---presynaptic activity, an output-side teaching factor, leaky
state, and no error path across depth---are structurally compatible with
layer-local or event-driven execution.  \TeReL{}-S further removes the temporal
graph, and our samplewise experiment shows that this stricter form can retain
useful accuracy.  These are architectural reasons to investigate specialized
implementations, not measurements of energy efficiency.  Low-rank lateral
state, fused state updates, and optimizer choices designed for local arrays are
the concrete engineering steps needed to test that perspective.

\paragraph{Recurrent extension.}
The temporal objective also suggests a recurrent representation module whose
slow state supports prediction without receiving prediction gradients.  The
earlier exploratory model did not enforce that separation and therefore cannot
test the idea cleanly.  A valid recurrent comparison should isolate the
representation and prediction paths, include matched random-state and BPTT
controls, and repeat across seeds.  We retain this as a motivated extension of
the mechanism, not as a result established here.

\section{Conclusion}

Temporal coherence, variance preservation, and decorrelation form a useful
representation-learning recipe that connects modern regularized
self-supervision to SFA.  \TeReL{} turns that recipe into a concrete deep local
rule: each nonlinear layer receives a temporal error, a gated neuron-local
expansion signal, and a same-layer decorrelation signal.  Canonical \TeReL{}
keeps short within-chunk temporal credit while cutting the error path across
depth.  \TeReL{}-S additionally detaches time and has stream-length-independent
state, although its dense lateral operator is quadratic in width.

After restoring the intended optimizer, schedule, normalization, and lateral
timescale, canonical \TeReL{} reaches 97.30\% on MNIST, within 1.04 points of
data-presentation-matched BP and 1.95 points above the BatchNorm-calibrated
random encoder.  The final
layer alone retains 97.14\%, while temporally local \TeReL{}-S reaches 96.29\%
and its tuned samplewise form learns a high-rank 95.14\% validation
representation.  Matched Local SupCon reaches 96.98\%, 0.32 points below
\TeReL{} across the paired seeds.  One-factor controls further show that temporal coherence
supplies the aligned signal, variance expansion prevents scale collapse, and
decorrelation prevents redundancy collapse.  These results substantiate the
paper's central intuition in a controlled label-ordered setting: temporal
coherence, protected against collapse and redundancy, can train a
two-hidden-layer representation with local credit assignment.  Whether that
advantage transfers to natural streams remains unresolved; the secondary
result and explicit cost accounting mark the next experiments without diluting
the controlled finding.

\section{Gradient derivations}\label{app:gradients}

For valid detached pairs,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_t}
\frac{1}{N_ID}\sum_s I_s\|z_s-\stopgrad(z_{s-1})\|^2
=\frac{2I_t}{N_ID}(z_t-p_t).
\end{equation}
Because $g$, $m$, $A$, and $q_t$ are detached,
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial L_V}{\partial z_t}
&=-\frac{2}{BD}g\odot(z_t-m),\\
\frac{\partial L_C}{\partial z_t}
&=\frac{1}{BD}Aq_t.
\end{align}
Combining terms gives \cref{eq:local-gradient}.  Applying the chain rule first
through $\phi$ and then through the minibatch map $N_{\eta,\mathcal B}$ gives
the Jacobian-block form in \cref{eq:weight-gradient}; setting $N$ to the
identity recovers the displayed presynaptic outer-product special case.  For undetached adjacent
pairs, $z_t$ appears as the current member of pair $t$ and the previous member
of pair $t+1$; summing those derivatives gives \cref{eq:batch-gradient}.
Finite-fixture regression tests check both signs and the presence of the future
term.

For the direct covariance control, let $Z_c$ stack centered rows and
$C=\offdiag(Z_c^\top Z_c/B)$.  Since $C$ is symmetric,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_c}\frac{\|C\|_F^2}{D}
=\frac{4}{BD}Z_cC,
\end{equation}
equivalently $4C(z_t-m)/(BD)$ in column-vector notation.  The proxy replaces
$4C(z_t-m)$ by the detached direction $Aq_t$ and absorbs the positive scale
into $\lambda_C$.

\section{Implementation-fidelity tests}\label{app:tests}

The public suite checks:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the negative variance gradient sign, variance gate, temporal
  detachment, undetached future derivative, lateral-proxy error bound, and
  direct covariance fixture;
  \item nonzero updates for every declared encoder layer and lateral state,
  with no gradient from a later layer to an earlier layer;
  \item fixed dynamic-state size, bounded streaming normalization, samplewise
  gradient release, and absence of retained autograd graphs;
  \item train-only BatchNorm calibration that changes running buffers while
  leaving every random-encoder weight, bias, and affine parameter unchanged;
  \item exact MNIST split roles, PAMAP2 subject roles, train-only
  normalization, temporal boundaries, and feature/boundary-only encoder batches;
  \item matched all-layer and last-layer probes, greedy layerwise scheduling,
  execution of every declared BP layer, and exact data-pass accounting;
  \item batch SFA constraints, boundary-aware derivatives, and numerical
  validation of the official-algorithm IncSFA port; and
  \item refusal of held-out evaluation after protocol, ledger, commit, or
  tracked-worktree drift.
\end{itemize}
